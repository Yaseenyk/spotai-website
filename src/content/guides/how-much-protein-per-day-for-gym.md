Protein gives your muscles the material to repair and grow after training. Most gym-goers in India either guess their intake or follow a number from a supplement label. A simple target based on your body weight works better.

## The number: grams per kg of body weight

There are two figures worth knowing:

- **0.83 g per kg a day** is India's recommended amount for healthy adults (ICMR-NIN, 2020). It covers basic needs, not muscle building.
- **1.4 to 2 g per kg a day** is the range sports nutrition research suggests for people who train regularly (International Society of Sports Nutrition, 2017).

A large review of strength-training studies found that extra protein helped muscle growth up to about **1.6 g per kg a day**, with little added benefit beyond that on average (Morton et al., 2018). So 1.6 g per kg is a sensible target for most people. Going up to 2 g per kg is fine, but not essential.

| Body weight | Basic (0.83 g/kg) | Gym target (1.6 g/kg) | Upper range (2 g/kg) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50 kg | 42 g | 80 g | 100 g |
| 60 kg | 50 g | 96 g | 120 g |
| 70 kg | 58 g | 112 g | 140 g |
| 80 kg | 66 g | 128 g | 160 g |
| 90 kg | 75 g | 144 g | 180 g |

If you are just starting and currently eat 40 to 50 g a day, do not jump straight to 120 g. Add 15 to 20 g a day for two weeks, see how it fits, then add more.

### If you are carrying extra weight

Use your goal weight, not your current weight. Say you weigh 95 kg and want to reach 75 kg. Calculating from 95 kg gives about 150 g a day, which is hard to eat and more than you need. Using 75 kg gives about 120 g, which is easier to reach and still supports your training.

## Spread it across the day

Your body uses protein better in several moderate meals than in one big one. The ISSN suggests about 20 to 40 g of protein per meal, every three to four hours.

For a target of 100 g, that looks like:

- **Breakfast:** 25 g
- **Lunch:** 25–30 g
- **Evening snack or after gym:** 15–20 g
- **Dinner:** 25–30 g

The typical Indian pattern is a light breakfast, a carb-heavy lunch and a late dinner. Breakfast is usually where you lose the most protein, so fix that first.

## A sample day: about 105 to 125 g of protein

This is an example for a 70 kg person who eats eggs and chicken. If you are vegetarian, see our [high-protein Indian vegetarian diet](/guides/high-protein-indian-vegetarian-diet-for-gym/).

| Meal | What to eat | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breakfast | Omelette (1 whole egg + 3 whites) with 2 toast, 1 glass milk | about 28 g |
| Lunch | 2 rotis, chicken curry (120 g chicken), 1 katori dal, salad | about 38 g |
| Evening | Roasted chana and peanuts (40 g) or a glass of milk | about 10 g |
| Dinner | Rice, fish curry (120 g fish), 1 katori curd | about 30 g |
| Before bed (optional) | 100 g paneer bhurji or 1 glass milk | about 8–18 g |

## Mistakes that keep you short

1. **Counting roti and rice as your protein.** They add some, but a plate of three rotis and sabzi gives you only about 10 to 12 g.
2. **A small katori of dal.** One small katori is 6 to 7 g. Two full katoris make a real difference.
3. **Big protein at dinner only.** 60 g at night and almost nothing at breakfast is harder to use well and harder to keep up.
4. **Relying on a shake to fix everything.** Protein powder is a convenience. If you hit your target from food, you do not need it.
5. **Guessing.** Most people overestimate their protein. Write down what you eat for three ordinary days and add it up. You can do it on paper or in the SpotAI food log, which has Indian foods and totals protein for the day.

## Balance matters too

Protein works alongside enough total calories, carbohydrates for training energy, vegetables, water and sleep. If you are trying to lose fat, keeping protein high helps you hold on to muscle while you eat less. For calorie values of everyday dishes, see our guide to [calories in Indian food](/guides/calories-in-indian-food/).

If you have kidney problems, diabetes, are pregnant, or have any medical condition, check your protein target with your doctor or a registered dietitian first.
