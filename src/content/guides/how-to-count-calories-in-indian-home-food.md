Calorie apps were built for packaged food with a label. Indian home food does not work that way. One pot of dal feeds four people, every house uses a different amount of oil, and a "katori" can mean 100 ml or 250 ml. You can still count calories well enough to be useful. The trick is a method you can repeat, not a perfect number.

## Where most of the error comes from

Three things cause most counting mistakes in Indian food:

1. **Oil and ghee.** Fat has about 9 kcal per gram. One teaspoon of oil or ghee (about 5 g) is roughly 45 kcal, and a tablespoon is around 120 kcal. A tadka with three tablespoons, shared by four people, adds about 90 kcal to each bowl.
2. **Portion size.** Your katori, your serving spoon and your roti size matter more than which database you use.
3. **Cooked vs raw.** Rice and dal soak up water. 100 g of cooked rice is a very different amount of food from 100 g of raw rice.

Get these three right and the rest is small change.

## Step 1: learn your own portions once

Spend one week measuring, then stop.

- **Katori:** fill your usual katori with water and pour it into a measuring cup. Now you know if it is 150 ml or 250 ml.
- **Roti:** weigh the dough balls for one meal and divide. Most home rotis use 25–40 g of atta.
- **Rice:** weigh the raw rice you cook for one meal and note how many people eat it.
- **Oil:** pour your normal tadka into a measuring spoon once. Most people are surprised.

## Step 2: count shared dishes by the pot

For dal, sabzi, curry or khichdi cooked for the family:

1. Add up the raw ingredients that carry calories: dal or grain, oil or ghee, paneer, meat, coconut, cream, nuts. Ignore water, dry spices and most vegetables like tomato, onion, palak or lauki in normal amounts.
2. Divide by the number of servings you usually get from that pot.
3. Save it as "home dal, one katori" and reuse it.

Say you cook 200 g of raw toor dal (about 660 kcal) with 2 tablespoons of ghee (about 240 kcal) and get five katoris. Each katori is about 180 kcal. You only need to do this sum once for each regular dish.

## Rough numbers for common ingredients

These are approximate values per 100 g, rounded from the Indian Food Composition Tables. Use them for the raw ingredient sums above.

| Ingredient | Approx. kcal per 100 g | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole wheat atta | 320–340 | Count by dough weight |
| Raw rice | 345–360 | Cooked rice is roughly a third of this per 100 g |
| Toor, moong or masoor dal (raw) | 330–350 | Also gives 20–25 g protein |
| Paneer | about 260 | Varies with fat content |
| Oil or ghee | about 900 | The big one |
| Curd (whole milk) | about 60 | Low-fat curd is less |
| Egg (one large) | about 70–80 per egg | |

## Step 3: log the day in plates, not grams

Once your portions are known, logging takes two minutes:

| Meal | What you ate | Approx. kcal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breakfast | 2 rotis (30 g atta each), 1 tsp ghee, 1 katori curd | 200 + 45 + 90 = 335 |
| Lunch | 1 katori home dal, 1 cup cooked rice, 1 katori sabzi | 180 + 200 + 120 = 500 |
| Snack | Tea with milk and 1 tsp sugar, roasted chana (30 g) | 60 + 110 = 170 |
| Dinner | 2 rotis, 1 katori paneer sabzi, salad | 200 + 250 + 20 = 470 |
| **Total** | | **about 1,475** |

This is a hypothetical example, not a target. Your needs depend on your size, activity and goal.

## How accurate is accurate enough?

Your count can be 10–20% off and still be useful, as long as it is off in the same way every day. What matters is the trend:

- Log for two or three weeks.
- Weigh yourself a few mornings a week and look at the weekly average.
- If your weight is not changing and you want it to, cut 200–300 kcal a day, usually from oil, sugar or a second helping of rice.

The ICMR-NIN dietary guidelines describe losing about half a kilo a week as a safe pace, and say weight-loss diets should not go below 1,000 kcal a day. If you have diabetes, a thyroid condition or any other medical condition, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before cutting calories.

## Eating out and at relatives' homes

You will not get these right, and that is fine. Pick the closest dish, assume a bit more oil than at home, and move on. One estimated meal does not undo a week of careful logging.

## Do not forget protein

Counting calories alone can push people to cut dal, paneer and eggs because they "have calories". Keep them. If you train, protein helps you hold on to muscle while you lose fat. Our guide on [how much protein you need](/guides/how-much-protein-per-day-for-gym/) explains targets in grams.
