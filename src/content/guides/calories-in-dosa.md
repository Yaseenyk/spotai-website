A dosa is a thin pancake, so people assume it is light. By the numbers it sits between idli and a paratha, and the size and the oil move it more than the batter does. A plain dosa from a home tawa and a masala dosa the size of a plate are not the same food, and most pages give one calorie figure for both. This guide shows each by size, with the weights, and then the sides.

## Calories in dosa per 100 g

The ICMR-NIN Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017) list raw foods only, so they do not have the cooked dosa. The finished-dish figures here are from USDA's survey food database (FNDDS 2021 to 2023), which has "Dosa, plain" and "Dosa, with filling" with portion sizes.

| Per 100 g | Calories | Protein | Fat | Carbohydrate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dosa, plain | 210 kcal | 5.70 g | 4.05 g | 37.0 g |
| Dosa, with filling (masala) | 184 kcal | 5.46 g | 4.27 g | 30.8 g |

The masala dosa is lower per 100 g because the potato filling is wet and low in calories by weight. It is far higher per piece because the dosa is much bigger once the filling is in.

## Calories in 1 dosa by size

USDA lists portion weights for small, medium and large. We multiply them by the per-100 g values.

| Dosa | Weight (USDA) | Calories | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plain, small | 35 g | about 74 kcal | 2.0 g |
| Plain, medium | 80 g | about 168 kcal | 4.6 g |
| Plain, large | 155 g | about 326 kcal | 8.8 g |
| Masala, small | 113 g | about 208 kcal | 6.2 g |
| Masala, medium | 267 g | about 491 kcal | 14.6 g |
| Masala, large | 523 g | about 962 kcal | 28.6 g |

These weights are from a US database that describes dosas as small, medium and large, and the large masala dosa at 523 g is a big restaurant portion. Your tawa and your restaurant may be quite different. If you can, weigh a plain dosa from your own pan once and multiply by 210 kcal per 100 g. The 962 kcal for the large masala dosa is a calculation, not a claim that every large masala dosa has that much; treat it as an upper example.

## What the oil does

The fat in the table (about 4 g per 100 g) means some oil is already included in the USDA recipe. Dosas made with more oil or ghee on the tawa, a "ghee roast" or a crispy paper dosa, will be higher. USDA lists vegetable oil at about 884 kcal per 100 g and butter oil (ghee) at 876 kcal per 100 g, so a teaspoon of either (4.3 to 4.5 g) is about 37 to 40 kcal. Two extra teaspoons on a medium dosa add about 75 kcal, which is a 45 percent increase on 168 kcal.

## Dosa with sambar and chutney

Using the same sides as in the [calories in idli](/guides/calories-in-idli/) guide:

- Sambar: USDA lists "Sambar, vegetable stew" at 86 kcal and 4.33 g of protein per 100 g. Half a cup is 124 g, so about 107 kcal and 5.4 g.
- Coconut chutney: no table lists it as a dish. IFCT 2017 gives fresh coconut kernel at about 409 kcal and 3.8 g of protein per 100 g; 20 g (our assumption) is about 82 kcal and 0.8 g.

| Meal | Dosa | Sambar and chutney | Total | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 medium plain dosa | 168 kcal | 189 kcal | about 357 kcal | about 11 g |
| 2 medium plain dosas | 336 kcal | 189 kcal | about 525 kcal | about 15 g |
| 1 medium masala dosa | 491 kcal | 189 kcal | about 680 kcal | about 21 g |

The sides are a large part of the plain-dosa meal: more than half of it for one dosa. For a masala dosa the filling already carries most of the energy, and the sides are a smaller share. The NIN manual *Dietary Guidelines for Indians* (2011) lists 2 dosas in its sample plan for a sedentary woman and 3 in the plan for a sedentary man, as one of several breakfast options (the others include idli and upma). It does not say how big the dosas are, so we cannot turn that into a calorie figure.

## Where the batter's protein comes from

Dosa batter is usually rice and urad dal. IFCT 2017 gives raw parboiled rice at about 352 kcal and 7.81 g of protein per 100 g and raw urad dal at about 324 kcal and 23.06 g of protein per 100 g. USDA's 5.7 g of protein per 100 g of finished plain dosa is higher than cooked rice (about 2.7 g), which fits a batter that has urad dal in it. We do not know your ratio, so use the USDA figure for the finished dosa and weigh yours.

## How to count your own dosa

1. Weigh one cooked dosa. For a masala dosa, weigh it with the filling if you eat both.
2. Multiply by 210 kcal per 100 g for plain, or 184 kcal per 100 g with filling.
3. Add extra ghee or oil at 37 to 40 kcal a teaspoon, and the sambar and chutney.
4. If you eat rice at lunch, see [calories in rice](/guides/calories-in-rice/) so you use the same method for both.

For a method to count dishes cooked at home in bulk, read [how to count calories in Indian home food](/guides/how-to-count-calories-in-indian-home-food/).

All values are estimates. Dosas differ with batter, pan, oil and filling, and this page is food data, not diet advice.
