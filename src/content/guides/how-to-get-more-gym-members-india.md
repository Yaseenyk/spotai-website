When a gym is not growing, the instinct is to print more pamphlets or run a big discount. Usually the leak is elsewhere: people find the gym, ask about fees, and never hear back. Or they join, train for six weeks, and quietly stop. Fix those first, then spend on marketing.

## Know where members come from

For one month, ask every new enquiry one question: "How did you hear about us?" Write it down. Then track each step.

| Step | What to count each week | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enquiries | Calls, WhatsApp messages, walk-ins | Is your gym being found? |
| Visits | People who came to see the gym | Is your follow-up working? |
| Trials | People who did a session | Is the visit convincing? |
| Joins | People who paid | Is your offer and price right? |
| Still coming at 60 days | New members active after two months | Will they renew? |

Say you get 40 enquiries a month but only 8 visits. More advertising will not help much. Faster follow-up will.

## 1. Make your Google listing work

For a neighbourhood gym, most new members search "gym near me" or "gym in [area]" on Google Maps. A free Google Business Profile is the most important page you have.

- Use your real gym name, exact location pin and phone number that someone answers.
- Add opening hours, including ladies' batch timings and Sunday hours.
- Upload recent photos of the floor, equipment, changing rooms and parking.
- Mention your monthly and quarterly fees, or at least a starting price. People skip gyms with no price.
- **Ask for reviews the right way.** Ask happy members directly, a week or two after they join. Do not pay for reviews or give discounts in exchange for them: Google's policies prohibit incentivised reviews, and India's standard on online consumer reviews (IS 19000:2022) says reviews must be genuine.
- Reply to every review, including the bad ones, politely and briefly.

## 2. Reply the same day

An enquiry is warm for a day or two. After that, the person has joined somewhere else or lost interest.

- Reply to every WhatsApp message and missed call **within a few hours**.
- Answer the fee question directly. Hiding the price until they visit puts people off.
- Offer a specific time: "Come at 7 pm tomorrow, Rahul will show you around."
- If they visit but do not join, send one message the next day. Then one more after a week. Then stop.

## 3. Give trials a plan

A free day pass with no guidance often ends with someone walking on the treadmill alone and never returning. Make the trial a short, guided session:

- A trainer meets them at a fixed time.
- They do four or five simple exercises with form checks.
- They leave with a simple first-week plan, whether they join or not.
- A follow-up message the next day asks how they feel.

## 4. Turn members into referrals

Your current members already know people who want to start. Give them a reason to bring them.

- **Reward both sides.** For example, two free weeks for the member and a waived registration fee for the friend, given only after the friend pays.
- **Make it easy.** A ready WhatsApp message they can forward with your location and fees.
- **Ask at the right moment.** Right after a member hits a personal best or completes a month is a better time than at the payment counter.

## 5. Local tie-ups

| Partner | Idea |
| --- | --- |
| Offices and IT parks nearby | A corporate rate for five or more employees joining together |
| Colleges and coaching centres | Student plans at off-peak hours |
| Apartment societies | A free form-check or posture session in the clubhouse |
| Physiotherapists and clinics | They refer clients ready for gym training; you refer injuries to them |
| Sports shops and cafés | Shared offers and posters |

## 6. Time your push

Many people decide to start at familiar moments: New Year, after Diwali, before a family wedding, before summer. Check your own register for the months when joins went up, and plan offers and posts two to three weeks ahead of them.

## 7. Social media and WhatsApp without spam

- On Instagram, show real members (with their permission), real equipment and short form tips. Avoid stock photos.
- Only message people on WhatsApp who have shared their number with you and agreed to hear from you. WhatsApp's business policy requires opt-in, and broadcasts to strangers get numbers blocked.
- Do not promise results like "lose 10 kg in 30 days". India's guidelines on misleading advertisements (2022) apply to fitness claims too, and the promise sets members up to be disappointed.

## 8. Keep the members you get

Growth is joins minus leavers. A gym that signs up 30 members a month but loses 25 is barely growing. Put the same effort into the first month of each new member: a plan, a check-in habit, and a message when they miss a week.
