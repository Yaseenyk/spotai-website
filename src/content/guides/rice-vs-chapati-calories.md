"Rice or chapati?" is asked in every Indian home that counts calories, and the answers online contradict each other. Most of the confusion is that people compare different things: raw against cooked, 100 g against a portion. This guide lines them up in four ways, so you can see which comparison gives which answer. The single-food guides, [calories in rice](/guides/calories-in-rice/) and [calories in roti and chapati](/guides/calories-in-roti-chapati/), have the working.

## Comparison 1: per 100 g of the cooked food

| Cooked, per 100 g | Calories | Protein | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White rice, cooked (long-grain) | 130 kcal | 2.7 g | USDA SR Legacy |
| Roti (chapati) | 299 kcal | 7.9 g | USDA FNDDS |

On this measure chapati has about 2.3 times the calories of rice. It is also the comparison that misleads most, because rice is steamed with water and roti is not. 100 g of roti is a small stack, and 100 g of rice is a small katori. Nobody eats equal weights of the two.

## Comparison 2: per 100 g of the raw grain

IFCT 2017 gives raw values, and converts to kJ, which we turned into kcal at 4.184 kJ per kcal.

| Raw, per 100 g | Calories | Protein | Total fibre |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raw milled rice | 356 kcal | 7.94 g | 2.81 g |
| Whole wheat atta | 320 kcal | 10.57 g | 11.36 g |

Before the water goes in, the two are within about 10 percent of each other on calories, rice slightly higher. Atta has more protein and about four times the fibre. This is the fairest like-for-like comparison, and it is also the way to count if you weigh the dry rice and the atta before cooking.

## Comparison 3: per portion

Here we have to make assumptions, and we state them. A medium roti is 40 g (USDA, 7 inch). A roti from 30 g of atta is a plainer, smaller version. For rice, a 250 ml bowl holds about 167 g, and a katori of 150 ml about 100 g, from USDA's cup weight of 158 g for cooked long-grain white rice. These weights are assumptions, since your bowls differ.

| Portion | Calories | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 roti, 30 g atta (IFCT) | about 96 kcal | 3.2 g |
| 2 rotis, 30 g atta each | about 192 kcal | 6.3 g |
| 1 medium roti, 40 g (USDA) | about 120 kcal | 3.1 g |
| 2 medium rotis, 40 g each (USDA) | about 239 kcal | 6.3 g |
| 1 katori cooked rice, 150 ml, about 100 g | about 130 kcal | 2.7 g |
| 1 US cup cooked rice, 158 g (USDA) | about 205 kcal | 4.3 g |
| 1 bowl cooked rice, 250 ml, about 167 g | about 217 kcal | 4.5 g |

The pattern: two rotis and one bowl of rice come out at about 190 to 240 kcal either way. Plates differ more than staples do. A heaped bowl of 250 g of cooked rice is about 325 kcal, about the same as 2.7 medium rotis. The argument is mostly about how much goes on the plate. The per-portion numbers are the ones to count, and the way to count them is in [how to count calories in Indian home food](/guides/how-to-count-calories-in-indian-home-food/).

## Comparison 4: per 100 calories

This one answers "what do I get for my calories".

| Per 100 kcal | Weight | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cooked white rice | about 77 g | about 2.1 g |
| Roti (USDA) | about 33 g | about 2.6 g |

So per 100 kcal, roti brings about a quarter more protein, and it is a lot less food by weight: 33 g against 77 g. If you are the kind of eater who feels a bowl of rice is a more filling quantity, that is the reason. We do not have data on fullness, so we say nothing more than the weights.

## Fibre

The clearest nutrient gap in the tables is fibre. IFCT 2017 puts raw whole wheat atta at 11.36 g of total dietary fibre per 100 g and raw milled rice at 2.81 g. Raw brown rice has 4.43 g and raw parboiled rice 3.74 g. For 60 g of raw grain, that is about 6.8 g from atta against about 1.7 g from milled rice. Cooking and refining change this somewhat, but the order stays.

## Brown rice

Brown rice is closer to chapati than white rice on protein and fibre, and about the same on calories. IFCT lists raw brown rice at about 354 kcal, 9.16 g of protein and 4.43 g of fibre per 100 g. USDA lists cooked brown rice at 123 kcal and 2.7 g of protein per 100 g, against 130 kcal for cooked white rice. It is a small step, not a swap that changes a day.

## Adding ghee, oil and dal

Most of the difference between a rice meal and a roti meal on your plate is what goes with it. Ghee on roti and in rice adds about 37 kcal a teaspoon (USDA). Roti is often served with a dry sabzi and rice with a wet dal or curry, which carry their own oil. Count the sides separately. The [7-day diet plan for weight loss](/guides/7-day-diet-plan-for-weight-loss/) shows how the staples sit in a full day.

## The honest conclusion

- Per 100 g cooked, chapati has more than twice the calories of rice. That is not a useful comparison.
- Per 100 g raw, rice has about 10 percent more calories than atta.
- Per portion, 2 rotis and a bowl of rice are about equal.
- Chapati has more protein and fibre per calorie.
- Your portion size and the fat you add change the result more than the choice does.

This is a comparison of food data, not diet advice. If you manage blood sugar or another condition, ask a doctor or dietitian which staple and how much suits you.
