"Calories in rice" has many different answers online because the question is missing a word: raw or cooked. 100 g of raw rice and 100 g of cooked rice are very different amounts of food. This page gives both, then turns the cooked numbers into the portions you actually eat: a katori, a bowl, a cup. Every figure comes from a named table, and the gram weight of each portion is shown, because that is where most of the doubt sits.

## Calories in rice per 100 g

| Rice, per 100 g | Calories (kcal) | Protein (g) | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White, cooked (long-grain) | 130 | 2.7 | USDA FoodData Central |
| White, cooked (medium-grain) | 130 | 2.4 | USDA FoodData Central |
| Parboiled, cooked | 123 | 2.9 | USDA FoodData Central |
| Brown, cooked | 123 | 2.7 | USDA FoodData Central |
| Raw milled rice | 356 | 7.9 | ICMR-NIN IFCT 2017 |
| Raw parboiled rice | 352 | 7.8 | ICMR-NIN IFCT 2017 |
| Raw brown rice | 354 | 9.2 | ICMR-NIN IFCT 2017 |

The Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017) report energy in kilojoules and only for raw foods. We converted at 4.184 kJ per kcal: raw milled rice is 1,491 kJ, which is 356 kcal. The cooked figures come from USDA, which measures cooked rice (boiled, no salt or fat added). Rice cooked in your kitchen can be a little wetter or drier, so expect small differences.

## Raw vs cooked: why the numbers are so far apart

Rice soaks up water when it cooks. The grain itself does not gain calories, it only gets heavier. That is why 100 g of raw rice is about 356 kcal but 100 g of cooked rice is about 130 kcal.

Dividing one by the other gives a rough idea: 356 kcal of rice, once cooked, weighs about 275 g (356 divided by 1.30 kcal per gram). In other words, 100 g of raw rice makes a little under 300 g of cooked rice. This is a derived figure, and real water uptake changes with the variety, the soak and the method, so use it as a guide only.

For counting, this means:

- If you weigh **raw** rice before cooking, use about 356 kcal per 100 g. 50 g of raw rice is about 178 kcal, and 75 g is about 267 kcal.
- If you weigh **cooked** rice, use about 130 kcal per 100 g. 500 g of cooked rice is about 650 kcal.
- Never mix the two. Weighing cooked rice and multiplying by the raw figure makes the count about three times too high.

## Calories in 1 katori, 1 bowl and 1 cup of cooked rice

Katori and bowl sizes are not standard. Our table assumes a katori of 150 ml and a bowl of 250 ml. We worked out the grams from USDA's weight for one cup of cooked long-grain white rice (158 g in a 236.6 ml cup, which is about 0.67 g per ml) and then 130 kcal per 100 g. The NIN Dietary Guidelines manual uses a cup of 200 ml for its sample meals, so we show that too.

| Portion of cooked white rice | Weight used | Calories | Protein | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 katori, 150 ml, level | about 100 g | about 130 kcal | about 2.7 g | Assumed volume, USDA density |
| 1 cup, 200 ml (NIN sample meals) | about 134 g | about 174 kcal | about 3.6 g | Assumed volume, USDA density |
| 1 US cup (USDA, long-grain) | 158 g | about 205 kcal | about 4.3 g | USDA portion weight |
| 1 bowl, 250 ml | about 167 g | about 217 kcal | about 4.5 g | Assumed volume, USDA density |
| 1 US cup, medium-grain (stickier) | 186 g | about 242 kcal | about 4.4 g | USDA portion weight |

Two things to take from this table. First, "1 bowl of cooked rice" is somewhere between about 175 and 240 kcal depending on the bowl and how firmly the rice is packed. Second, heaping matters: a heaped katori holding 150 g of cooked rice is about 195 kcal, half as much again as a level one.

If you eat from a plate rather than a measured vessel, the plate is the biggest unknown. The kitchen scale is the cure: weigh the cooked rice on your plate once or twice, and you will know your normal portion for the rest of the year. The method is in [how to count calories in Indian home food](/guides/how-to-count-calories-in-indian-home-food/).

## Adding ghee, oil and other things

Plain boiled rice has almost no fat. Jeera rice, pulao and fried rice do. USDA lists clarified butter (butter oil) at 876 kcal per 100 g and a tablespoon at 12.8 g, so a teaspoon (a third of that, about 4.3 g) is roughly 37 kcal. A tablespoon of ghee over a katori of dal rice is about 112 kcal on its own. Vegetable oil is about 884 kcal per 100 g, close to the same.

Dal, curd and sabzi on the side are counted separately. The [calories in Indian food chart](/guides/calories-in-indian-food/) has more finished dishes.

## Does rice have protein?

Yes, but not much per portion. A katori of cooked rice has about 2.7 g of protein, so rice supports a meal rather than carries it. IFCT 2017 puts raw milled rice at 7.94 g per 100 g, and brown rice slightly higher at 9.16 g. If you are trying to reach a protein target, the usual fix is a larger portion of dal, curd, eggs or paneer beside the rice, not more rice. The [highest protein foods in India](/guides/highest-protein-foods-in-india/) list shows which foods give the most per 100 g.

## What to do with these numbers

- Decide whether you will count rice raw or cooked, and stay with it.
- Weigh a typical katori or plate of your own rice a few times. Use that weight, not a general one.
- Add the ghee or oil separately.
- Treat all figures as estimates. Portions, varieties and cooking change the real value, and a count that is 10 to 20 percent off in the same direction every day is still useful.

This page gives food composition data, not diet advice. If you have diabetes or another medical condition, ask a doctor or a registered dietitian how much rice is right for you.
