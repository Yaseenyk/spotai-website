The lat pulldown is one of the first back exercises most beginners learn, and one of the most commonly done badly. You see people leaning back like they are rowing a boat, yanking the stack with their arms. Done well, it builds a wider, stronger back and prepares you for pull-ups.

## Muscles it works

- **Latissimus dorsi (lats):** the large, wing-shaped muscles on the sides of your back. They do most of the work.
- **Middle back (rhomboids, middle trapezius):** pull your shoulder blades together and down.
- **Rear shoulders:** help pull your arms back.
- **Biceps and forearms:** help bend the elbows and hold the bar.

## Machine setup

1. **Thigh pad.** Adjust it so your thighs are snug underneath with your feet flat on the floor. This stops you lifting off the seat.
2. **Weight.** Set the pin in the stack. Start light the first time.
3. **Bar.** The standard long bar with angled ends is fine for most people.
4. **Seat position.** Sit facing the machine, directly under the bar, so it comes straight down in front of your face.

## How to do a lat pulldown, step by step

1. **Grip.** Stand, take the bar with an overhand grip a little wider than your shoulders, and sit down, letting your arms straighten fully overhead.
2. **Set your chest.** Lift your chest and lean back slightly, about 10 to 20 degrees. Keep your lower back in its natural curve.
3. **Start with the shoulders.** Pull your shoulder blades down, away from your ears, before your elbows bend.
4. **Pull.** Drive your elbows down and back towards your ribs. Bring the bar to your upper chest, around the collarbone.
5. **Pause.** Hold for a moment with your chest up and shoulder blades squeezed.
6. **Return slowly.** Let the bar rise over about two seconds until your arms are straight and you feel a stretch in your lats. Do not let the stack slam.
7. **Breathe.** Breathe out as you pull, in as the bar goes up.

## Common mistakes and how to fix them

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leaning far back | Torso swings back on every rep, like a row | Lower the weight; keep the lean small and fixed |
| Using momentum | Jerking the bar down, bouncing at the top | Slow the lowering to 2 seconds, pause at the bottom |
| Pulling with the arms | Biceps burn, back feels nothing | Lighter weight, loose grip, think "elbows to back pockets" |
| Shrugging | Shoulders creep up to the ears | Pull shoulder blades down before each rep |
| Half reps | Bar stops at the chin, arms never straighten | Full stretch at the top, bar to upper chest at the bottom |
| Behind the neck | Head pushed forward, bar behind | Pull to the front of the chest |
| Lifting off the seat | Hips rise as you pull | Tighten the thigh pad; lower the weight |

## Grip options

| Grip | How | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard overhand | Palms forward, a little wider than shoulders | The default. Good all-round back exercise |
| Close neutral grip | V-handle, palms facing each other | Many people find it comfortable on the shoulders |
| Underhand | Palms facing you, shoulder width | More biceps involvement |
| Very wide | Hands far out on the bar | Shorter range of motion; not better for most people |

One study that measured muscle activity found that pulling to the front of the chest with a wide grip worked the lats more than pulling behind the neck. For most beginners, the best grip is the one that feels comfortable and lets you control the weight.

## Sets, reps and how heavy

- **Beginners:** 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps, resting about 60 to 90 seconds.
- **Weight:** choose a load where the last two reps are hard but you do not need to lean back or jerk the bar.
- **Progress:** when you can do 3 x 12 with good form, move the pin down one plate. Drop back to 3 x 8 or 10 and build up again.

Our guide on [sets and reps for muscle](/guides/how-many-sets-and-reps-to-build-muscle/) explains how many total sets a week to aim for.

Weight stacks differ a lot between machines, so the number on the plate at your gym may not match another gym's. Track your progress on the same machine.

## Where it fits in your week

The lat pulldown is a "pull" exercise. Pair it with a push like the dumbbell bench press or shoulder press in a full-body day, or put it at the start of a back or pull day. In a [push, pull, legs split](/guides/push-pull-legs-split-for-beginners/), it goes on pull day.

## Check your form on video

Ask a friend to film one set, or prop your phone at shoulder height to your side, about 2 metres away. Look for:

- How far you lean back at the start versus the last rep
- Whether the bar reaches your upper chest every time
- Whether your arms fully straighten at the top
- Whether your shoulders creep up

## From pulldowns to pull-ups

The pulldown builds the strength for pull-ups. You do not need to wait until you can pull your body weight on the stack. Start practising early:

- **Assisted pull-ups** on the machine or with a band
- **Slow negatives:** jump to the top and lower yourself over 3 to 5 seconds
- **Dead hangs** for 20 to 30 seconds to build grip

Keep the pulldown in your plan even after you can do pull-ups. It is easy to adjust and great for extra back work.

If you feel pain in your shoulder, elbow or neck during pulldowns, stop and see a doctor or physiotherapist before continuing.
