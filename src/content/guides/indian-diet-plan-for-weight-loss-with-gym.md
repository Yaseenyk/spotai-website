Most "Indian diet plans for weight loss" online are a fixed chart: 1,200 calories, a list of meals, and no explanation. They ignore your size, your training and the food your family cooks. A better approach is a set of simple rules that work with your normal meals and adjust as you go.

## The basic rules

1. **A small deficit.** Eat a little less than you burn. ICMR-NIN's 2024 guidelines describe about 0.5 kg a week as a safe rate of loss, and say diets should not go below 1,000 kcal a day.
2. **Protein in every meal.** It helps you hold on to muscle while you lose fat, and keeps you full.
3. **Keep lifting.** Strength training tells your body to keep muscle. Without it, more of the weight you lose can come from muscle.
4. **Measure, then adjust.** Your weight trend over two weeks tells you more than any calculator.

## How much to eat

Skip the online formulas for now. Do this instead:

1. For one week, eat normally and log what you eat. Our guide on [counting calories in Indian home food](/guides/how-to-count-calories-in-indian-home-food/) shows how to do this with shared dishes.
2. Weigh yourself 3–4 mornings that week, after the toilet, before food. Note the average.
3. If your weight held steady, that intake is roughly your maintenance. Cut 300–500 kcal a day from it.

Most of that cut usually comes from three places: cooking oil, sugar in tea and sweets, and second helpings of rice or roti.

## Build each plate the same way

Use your own katori and plate. For lunch and dinner:

- **Half the plate: vegetables.** Sabzi, salad, kosambari, raita with cucumber.
- **A quarter: protein.** Dal, paneer, chana, rajma, eggs, chicken, fish, curd.
- **A quarter: grains.** One cup of cooked rice, or two medium rotis, or one of each if they are small.
- **Oil:** about one teaspoon of oil or ghee per person per dish, not a ladle.

This works for South Indian, North Indian and most home cooking. It also keeps everyone at the table eating the same food.

## How much protein

| Guideline | Amount | Who it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICMR-NIN RDA | 0.83 g per kg body weight per day | General adult population |
| ISSN position stand | 1.4–2.0 g per kg body weight per day | People who exercise regularly |

So a 70 kg person who lifts might aim for roughly 100–140 g a day. That is a lot for a typical Indian diet. Spread it out: 25–35 g at each main meal and a protein snack. See [how much protein you need per day](/guides/how-much-protein-per-day-for-gym/) for more detail. If you have kidney disease or another medical condition, ask your doctor before raising protein.

## A sample vegetarian day

| Meal | What to eat | Approx. protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breakfast | Moong dal chilla (2), with mint chutney, 1 katori curd | 15–18 g |
| Lunch | 1 cup rice, 1 katori dal, 1 katori sabzi, salad | 10–12 g |
| Snack | Roasted chana (30 g), tea with less sugar | 6–7 g |
| Dinner | 2 rotis, 100 g paneer bhurji or palak paneer, salad | 20–22 g |
| Before bed (optional) | 1 glass milk or a bowl of curd | 6–7 g |

That lands around 60–65 g. To get closer to a training target, add a second katori of dal, more paneer or tofu, soya chunks in the sabzi, or a scoop of whey.

## A sample non-vegetarian day

| Meal | What to eat | Approx. protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breakfast | 3-egg omelette with vegetables, 1 roti or 2 slices of bread | 20–22 g |
| Lunch | 1 cup rice, 150 g chicken or fish curry, sabzi, salad | 30–35 g |
| Snack | Sprouts chaat or a bowl of curd | 6–8 g |
| Dinner | 2 rotis, 1 katori dal, 1 katori sabzi | 12–14 g |

These are examples, not prescriptions. Change the dishes to what your family cooks; keep the structure.

## Training days and rest days

Keep it simple. Eat roughly the same each day, with the same protein. If you prefer, move one portion of rice or a banana to before or after your training session, and have a little less on rest days.

Keep your gym plan focused on strength, three days a week, with walking or cardio on other days. The WHO recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate activity a week and muscle-strengthening on at least two days.

## How to adjust every two weeks

Compare your average weight now with two weeks ago.

| Change in 2 weeks | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Lost 0.5–1.5 kg | Keep going |
| Lost less than 0.5 kg | Cut another 200 kcal a day, usually oil or rice |
| Lost more than 2 kg | Eat a little more; this is faster than the safe pace |
| Gained | Check portions and weekends before cutting further |

Also check your lifts. If your strength is dropping fast, your deficit is probably too big.

## What to avoid

- **Very low calorie plans** and fruit-only or juice-only days.
- **Skipping dal and paneer** because they "have calories". You need the protein.
- **Fat burners and detox teas.** They do not replace a deficit.
- **Weighing yourself once and panicking.** Water, salt and periods move the scale by a kilo or more in a day.

If you have diabetes, PCOS, a thyroid condition, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or take regular medicines, see a doctor or registered dietitian before changing your diet.
