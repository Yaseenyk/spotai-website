When a new gym struggles, it is rarely because the equipment was wrong. It is usually because the gym has not reached enough paying, regular members to cover rent and salaries. This guide covers the setup steps, but also the numbers and habits that keep a new gym open.

## 1. Decide what kind of gym you are opening

| Type | Typical space | Who it suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neighbourhood strength gym | Small to mid-size floor | Residential areas; members who want a nearby, affordable gym |
| Premium gym | Large floor, more cardio, amenities | High-income areas, offices nearby |
| Functional or CrossFit-style box | Open floor, fewer machines | Members who like group classes |
| Ladies-only or batch-based gym | Small to mid-size | Areas with demand for women-only timings |
| Franchise | As specified by the brand | Owners who want a known name and a set playbook |

Your type decides the space, equipment, staff and price, so choose it first.

## 2. Pick the location

Check these before you look at rent:

- **People nearby**: how many homes, hostels and offices are within a 10-minute drive or walk?
- **Competition**: what do nearby gyms charge, how busy are they at 7 pm, and what do their reviews complain about?
- **Access and parking** for two-wheelers in the evening rush.
- **Floor and building**: ground or first floor is easier for heavy equipment. Upper floors need a check of load and noise, and some buildings will not allow a gym.
- **Power and ventilation**: a backup supply, and enough AC or airflow for summer.
- **Lease terms**: lock-in period, rent escalation each year, and permission for fit-out.

## 3. Registrations and licences

Requirements differ by state and city. Confirm with your municipal office, state labour department and a CA.

| Registration | From | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business structure | MCA / bank | Proprietorship, partnership, LLP or private limited company |
| PAN and current account | Income Tax Dept / bank | In the business name |
| Udyam (MSME) registration | udyamregistration.gov.in | Free online; can help with loans and schemes |
| Shops and Establishments | State labour department | For example, the Telangana Shops and Establishments Act, 1988 |
| Trade licence | Municipal corporation | For example, GHMC in Hyderabad |
| Fire NOC | State fire service | Depends on building height, area and local rules |
| GST registration | GST portal | Required once turnover for services crosses ₹20 lakh a year (₹10 lakh in special category states); voluntary earlier |
| Music licences | PPL India, IPRS and similar bodies | Needed to play recorded music in a commercial space under the Copyright Act, 1957 |
| FSSAI registration | FSSAI | Only if you sell food, shakes or supplements |

## 4. GST: 5% without input tax credit

Since 22 September 2025, gym and fitness services carry 5% GST **without input tax credit**. Two things follow:

- **Your fee display should be clear.** Decide whether your price includes GST and say so on the board and the receipt.
- **GST you pay on purchases is a cost.** GST on equipment, fit-out, AC units and rent cannot be set off against the GST you collect. Budget for it in your setup costs.

## 5. List your cost heads

Get at least two quotes for each line in your city. Prices vary a lot and change often.

| One-time costs | Monthly costs |
| --- | --- |
| Security deposit and advance rent | Rent |
| Fit-out: flooring, mirrors, lighting, ACs, changing rooms | Salaries: trainers, front desk, cleaning |
| Strength equipment: racks, benches, dumbbells, plates, cables | Electricity and water |
| Cardio equipment | Equipment maintenance |
| Sound system, CCTV, reception | Software, phone, internet |
| Registrations and professional fees | Marketing |
| Opening marketing | Loan EMI, if any |

Used commercial equipment can cut the start cost. Have someone experienced check cables, bearings and welds before you buy.

## 6. Work out break-even before you sign

The number that matters is how many paying members you need each month.

> Break-even members = monthly costs ÷ average monthly fee per member (after GST)

Say, as a hypothetical example, your monthly costs come to ₹2,50,000, and your average member pays the equivalent of ₹1,500 a month after GST. You need about 167 members paying every month just to break even. If a third of members drop out within three months, you need to sign up more than that number to stay level.

Run this sum with your own quotes and local prices. Our guide on [pricing gym memberships in India](/guides/gym-membership-pricing-india/) covers how to set fees.

## 7. Staff and systems

- **Trainers**: at least one floor trainer at every busy hour. Check certifications and ask them to coach a sample session.
- **Front desk**: one person who knows every member's name is worth a lot.
- **Records**: attendance, dues and renewal dates in one place, on paper or in software, from day one.

## 8. Pre-launch

- Start taking enquiries and a waiting list a few weeks before opening.
- Offer a founding-member price for a fixed number of early sign-ups.
- Invite nearby housing societies, colleges and offices for a free trial week.
- Make sure your Google Business Profile is set up with correct timings and photos.

More ideas are in [how to get more gym members](/guides/how-to-get-more-gym-members-india/).

## 9. The first 90 days

New gyms often focus on sign-ups and forget the members who have already joined. In the first three months:

- **Give every new member a plan** and a trainer check in week one.
- **Watch who stops coming.** Message anyone who has not checked in for a week.
- **Collect fees on time** with polite reminders before the due date.
- **Fix complaints quickly**: AC, cleanliness, crowding at 7 pm.
