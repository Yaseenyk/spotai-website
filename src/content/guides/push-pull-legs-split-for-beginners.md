Push, pull, legs (PPL) is one of the most popular ways to split up gym training. It is simple to remember, and every day has a clear focus. The question for a beginner is not whether PPL works, but when to start it and how many days to train.

## What goes on each day

| Day | Muscles | Movement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Push** | Chest, shoulders (front and side), triceps | Pushing weight away from you |
| **Pull** | Back (lats, upper back), rear shoulders, biceps | Pulling weight towards you |
| **Legs** | Quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves | Squatting, hinging, lunging |

Because muscles that work together are trained on the same day, they get a full rest while you train the other days.

## Is it the right time for you?

PPL suits you if you can tick most of these:

- You have trained for **two to three months**, ideally on a full-body plan.
- You know how to do a squat, a press, a row and a hinge (like a Romanian deadlift) with decent form.
- You can get to the gym **three or more days a week** regularly.
- You are sleeping around seven hours or more most nights.

If you are brand new, start with our [beginner gym workout plan](/guides/gym-workout-plan-for-beginners/). Full-body sessions teach the movements faster because you practise each one three times a week.

## A 3-day PPL workout for beginners

Rest 2 to 3 minutes between sets on the first two exercises of each day, and 1 to 2 minutes on the rest. Stop each set with one or two good reps left.

### Day 1: Push

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
| --- | --- |
| Dumbbell or barbell bench press | 3 × 6–10 |
| Seated dumbbell shoulder press | 3 × 8–10 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 2 × 10–12 |
| Lateral raises | 3 × 12–15 |
| Triceps rope pushdown | 2 × 10–12 |

### Day 2: Pull

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
| --- | --- |
| Lat pulldown (or assisted pull-ups) | 3 × 8–10 |
| Seated cable row or one-arm dumbbell row | 3 × 8–12 |
| Face pulls | 2 × 12–15 |
| Dumbbell curls | 3 × 10–12 |
| Hammer curls | 2 × 10–12 |

### Day 3: Legs

| Exercise | Sets × reps |
| --- | --- |
| Goblet squat or barbell back squat | 3 × 6–10 |
| Romanian deadlift (dumbbells or barbell) | 3 × 8–10 |
| Leg press | 2 × 10–12 |
| Walking lunges | 2 × 10 per leg |
| Standing calf raises | 3 × 12–15 |

Add a plank (2 × 30–45 seconds) and dead bugs (2 × 10 per side) at the end of push and legs days.

Each session takes about 50 to 65 minutes including a short warm-up. Each muscle gets about 6 to 9 hard sets a week, which suits the first year. For how those numbers are set, see [how many sets and reps to build muscle](/guides/how-many-sets-and-reps-to-build-muscle/).

## Choosing your week

| Version | Layout | Each muscle trained | Who it suits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3-day | Push, rest, Pull, rest, Legs, rest, rest | Once a week | Beginners moving off full body |
| Rotating 4-day | Push, Pull, rest, Legs, Push, rest, Pull... | About 1.5× a week | Steady progress, 4 free days |
| 6-day | Push, Pull, Legs, Push, Pull, Legs, rest | Twice a week | Experienced, good sleep and food |

A review of training frequency found that training each muscle about **twice a week** led to more growth than once a week (Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger, 2016). So the 3-day version is a stepping stone. Once it feels comfortable, move to the rotating 4-day layout, where the days roll forward and each muscle is trained more often. Only go to 6 days if you can recover. Our guide to [rest days](/guides/rest-days-how-many-per-week/) covers the signs that you need more.

## When the gym is packed

Evening rush in Indian gyms means queues at the bench and squat rack. Swap, do not skip:

- **Bench press taken:** dumbbell press, machine chest press or push-ups with feet raised.
- **Squat rack taken:** goblet squat, leg press or Bulgarian split squats.
- **Lat pulldown taken:** one-arm dumbbell rows or a seated row machine.
- **No cable station:** dumbbell triceps extensions instead of pushdowns.

## Cardio on a PPL plan

Add 15 to 20 minutes of incline walking or cycling after pull or push days, or on a rest day. Keep hard cardio away from the day before legs so your legs are fresh.

## How to progress

Use double progression: when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets, add the smallest weight jump and drop back to the bottom of the range. Write your numbers down every session. ## Common mistakes

1. **Starting with 6 days** in week one and burning out by week four.
2. **Skipping leg day** because the queue is long or it is hard.
3. **Too many exercises.** Five or six per day is enough.
4. **No progress tracking.** If the weights in March match the weights in January, something needs to change.

If any exercise causes joint pain, stop and see a doctor or physiotherapist.
