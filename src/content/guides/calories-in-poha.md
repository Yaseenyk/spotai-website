Poha is easy to eat and hard to count. Nobody weighs it dry, the plate size changes from house to house, and the peanuts, potato and oil are different every time. Most "calories in poha" pages give one number and no working. Here the plate is built from its parts, so you can change any of them and see what happens.

## Poha per 100 g: dry flattened rice

The ICMR-NIN Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017) list "rice flakes" (poha) as a raw, dry food. Energy is given in kilojoules and we converted at 4.184 kJ per kcal.

| Dry poha, per 100 g | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calories | about 354 kcal (1,480 kJ) | IFCT 2017, A011 |
| Protein | 7.44 g | IFCT 2017, A011 |
| Fat | 1.14 g | IFCT 2017, A011 |
| Carbohydrate | 76.75 g | IFCT 2017, A011 |
| Total dietary fibre | 3.46 g | IFCT 2017, A011 |

A packaged thick poha in USDA's branded foods database lists 333 kcal and 6.7 g of protein per 100 g on its label (FDC 2265565), which is in the same range. Brands differ.

IFCT has no entry for cooked poha. Once washed and cooked, poha is mostly water plus whatever you fry it in, so "per 100 g of cooked poha" depends on the dish. That is why the plate method below works better.

## Calories in 1 plate of poha, built up

We use these values for the parts:

- Dry poha, 354 kcal and 7.44 g protein per 100 g (IFCT 2017).
- Cooking oil, 884 kcal per 100 g. A teaspoon is about 4.5 g, so about 40 kcal (USDA, peanut oil, 1 teaspoon = 4.5 g).
- Raw peanuts, 567 kcal and 25.8 g protein per 100 g (USDA). 10 g is about 57 kcal and 2.6 g of protein.
- Onion (big), 201 kJ per 100 g, about 48 kcal and 1.5 g protein (IFCT 2017). 30 g is about 14 kcal.
- Potato, 292 kJ per 100 g, about 70 kcal and 1.5 g protein (IFCT 2017, raw). 30 g is about 21 kcal.

| Plate | What is in it | Calories | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | 50 g dry poha, 1 teaspoon oil, 30 g onion | about 231 kcal | about 4.2 g |
| With peanuts and potato | The basic plate plus 10 g peanuts and 30 g potato | about 309 kcal | about 7.2 g |
| Large | 75 g dry poha, 2 teaspoons oil, 30 g onion, 15 g peanuts | about 444 kcal | about 9.9 g |

The weights in the first column of that table are our assumptions for a small, a medium and a large plate. They are not measured portions. If you want your own number, weigh the dry poha for your next plate, note the oil, and add up with the values above. Lemon juice, curry leaves, coriander and spices add very little.

Two things move the total most: the dry weight and the oil. Going from 50 g to 75 g of dry poha adds about 88 kcal. Going from one teaspoon of oil to two adds about 40 kcal. Most "heavy" poha is heavy because of those two things and the peanuts. See [how to count calories in Indian home food](/guides/how-to-count-calories-in-indian-home-food/) for how to handle oil when you cook for a family.

## Poha vs upma, for breakfast

USDA's survey database lists "Upma" at 87 kcal and 1.98 g of protein per 100 g, and a cup of cooked upma at 170 g. That makes a cup of upma about 148 kcal and 3.4 g of protein. Compared with the basic poha plate at about 231 kcal, that looks like a big gap, but the portions are not the same size and the oil is not the same. The US survey recipe for upma is a standard one; yours may use more ghee or more semolina. Count the dry weight of whatever you use.

The NIN Dietary Guidelines manual (2011) lists idli, dosa and upma (by the cup) as breakfast options in its sample meal plans, and poha (1 cup) as a snack option. It shows how people eat them, not a calorie figure for either.

## How to make poha higher in protein

Poha is a carbohydrate food with a little protein. If protein matters to you, the plate needs another source. Options from the tables above:

- **Peanuts.** 10 g adds about 2.6 g protein and 57 kcal. They are calorie-dense, so measure them.
- **Sprouts or a side of curd.** Curd has about 3.5 g of protein per 100 g (USDA plain whole-milk yogurt, 3.47 g per 100 g).
- **A boiled egg alongside.** USDA lists a hard-boiled egg at about 12.6 g of protein per 100 g, so a 50 g egg is about 6.3 g.

The [highest protein foods in India](/guides/highest-protein-foods-in-india/) list shows more options, and the [eggs vs paneer comparison](/guides/eggs-vs-paneer-protein/) gives the numbers for those two.

## What to take from this

- Dry poha is about 354 kcal per 100 g. Cooked poha has no standard number.
- A plate runs from about 230 kcal to over 440 kcal, depending on dry weight, oil and peanuts.
- Weigh the dry poha and measure the oil, and you can count it the same way every time.
- All values are estimates from tables and our assumptions about plate size. Portions vary.

This page gives food composition data, not diet advice.
