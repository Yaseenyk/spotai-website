Many women walk into a gym, look at the weights area full of men, and head straight to the treadmill. Cardio is fine, but strength training is where most of the change in shape, strength and confidence comes from. This plan keeps it simple: two workouts, three days a week, and a clear way to progress.

## Why lift weights at all

- **Strength for daily life.** Carrying shopping, lifting children, climbing stairs.
- **Shape.** Muscle gives a firmer look. Cardio alone rarely does that.
- **Bones and joints.** Strength training puts healthy load on bones and muscles. The WHO recommends muscle-strengthening activity on at least 2 days a week for all adults.
- **Confidence.** Watching your weights go up week by week is motivating.

The "bulky" worry is common and almost never comes true. Building large muscles takes years of heavy training and a big calorie surplus.

## The plan: two workouts, three days a week

Alternate Workout A and Workout B. For example:

- **Week 1:** Monday A, Wednesday B, Friday A
- **Week 2:** Monday B, Wednesday A, Friday B

### Workout A

| Exercise | Sets x reps | Muscles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goblet squat | 3 x 10 | Thighs, glutes |
| Lat pulldown | 3 x 10 | Back, arms |
| Dumbbell bench press | 3 x 10 | Chest, shoulders, arms |
| Glute bridge | 3 x 12 | Glutes, hamstrings |
| Seated cable row | 2 x 12 | Back |
| Plank | 2 x 20–30 seconds | Core |

### Workout B

| Exercise | Sets x reps | Muscles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leg press | 3 x 10 | Thighs, glutes |
| Dumbbell Romanian deadlift | 3 x 10 | Hamstrings, glutes, back |
| Seated dumbbell shoulder press | 3 x 10 | Shoulders |
| Walking lunges or split squats | 2 x 8 each leg | Thighs, glutes, balance |
| Assisted pull-up or band pulldown | 2 x 8 | Back |
| Dead bug | 2 x 8 each side | Core |

Rest about 60 to 90 seconds between sets. Each session should take 45 to 60 minutes with the warm-up.

The [goblet squat guide](/guides/goblet-squat-for-beginners/) shows how to do the first exercise in Workout A step by step.

## Warm-up (8–10 minutes)

- 5 minutes on the cycle, cross-trainer or brisk treadmill walk
- 10 bodyweight squats, 10 glute bridges, 10 arm circles each way
- One light set of your first exercise

## How to choose your weights

Pick a weight where the last 2 or 3 reps of each set feel hard, but your form still looks the same as the first rep.

- If you can easily do more than the top of the rep range, it is too light.
- If your form breaks before the bottom of the range, it is too heavy.

Write down what you lifted. When you can do all sets at the top of the range, for example 3 x 12, add the smallest step: 1 to 2.5 kg on dumbbells or one plate on a machine. This small, steady increase is called progressive overload, and it is how you get stronger.

## Where cardio fits

- **After weights:** 10 to 20 minutes of brisk walking on an incline, cycling or the cross-trainer.
- **On other days:** a 30-minute walk counts. The WHO suggests 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week in total.

If fat loss is your goal, food matters more than extra cardio. Strength training helps you keep muscle while you lose fat.

## Food and protein

You do not need a special diet, but most beginners do not eat enough protein. Simple Indian additions:

- **Breakfast:** curd with fruit, moong dal chilla, eggs or paneer bhurji
- **Lunch and dinner:** a full katori of dal or rajma or chana, not a small spoonful
- **Snacks:** roasted chana, sprouts, a glass of milk, boiled eggs

A common target for people who train is about 1.4 to 2 g of protein per kg of body weight a day. Our [protein guide](/guides/how-much-protein-per-day-for-gym/) shows how to work out yours.

Avoid very low-calorie diets while you start training. You will feel weak and are more likely to quit.

## Periods, pregnancy and health conditions

- **Periods.** Train as normal if you feel fine. On low days, lighten the weights or swap to walking and stretching. Painful or very heavy periods are worth discussing with a doctor.
- **Pregnancy or after childbirth.** The WHO encourages regular activity during pregnancy for women without complications, but get your doctor's advice before starting or changing a programme, and again after delivery.
- **PCOS, thyroid, back or knee problems.** Strength training is often part of a healthy routine, but check with your doctor or physiotherapist about what suits you.

## Feeling at ease at the gym

- **Ask about women-only batches** if your gym has them, at least for your first few weeks.
- **Ask a trainer, ideally a woman if you prefer, for a quick walkthrough** of each machine in your plan.
- **Go at a quieter time.** Mid-morning and early afternoon are often calmer than 6 to 9 pm.
- **Wear what lets you move freely.** Track pants, leggings or loose trousers and a t-shirt are all fine.
- **Bring a plan on your phone**, so you are not standing around deciding what to do.
- **Train with a friend** for the first month if that helps you go.

## Check your progress after 8 weeks

After about two months of three sessions a week, look at:

- Are your weights higher on each exercise than in week 1?
- Do stairs, carrying bags or getting up from the floor feel easier?
- Do your clothes fit differently?
- Did you make most of your sessions?

If yes, keep going and add weight slowly. If you missed many sessions, change the days or time rather than the plan.
