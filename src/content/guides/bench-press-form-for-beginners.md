The bench press is one of the best exercises for your chest, shoulders and triceps. It is also the lift beginners most often get wrong, usually because they copy someone lifting too heavy. Good form is simple once you know the setup.

## The setup: five points of contact

Before you touch the bar, get these five points right. This is the standard setup taught by the NSCA.

1. **Head** on the bench.
2. **Upper back and shoulders** on the bench, with shoulder blades squeezed together and pulled down, as if putting them into your back pockets.
3. **Hips/glutes** on the bench.
4. **Right foot** flat on the floor.
5. **Left foot** flat on the floor.

Lie so your **eyes are directly under the bar**. Too far back and you will hit the hooks when you press. Too far forward and the unrack becomes a long reach.

If your feet do not reach the floor comfortably, put a plate or step under each foot.

## Grip

- **Width**: a little wider than shoulder width. When the bar is on your chest, your forearms should be close to vertical when seen from the front.
- **Thumbs around the bar.** Never use a thumbless ("suicide") grip. The bar can roll out of your hands onto your chest or neck.
- **Wrists straight**, with the bar in the heel of your palm, not near your fingers.

## The lift, step by step

1. **Unrack.** Press the bar up off the hooks and move it over your shoulders with straight arms. Get your spotter to help if needed.
2. **Breathe in** and hold your breath lightly.
3. **Lower slowly**, taking about 2 seconds, to your lower chest. Elbows point out at roughly 45 to 70 degrees from your body, not flared straight out to the sides.
4. **Touch your chest lightly.** No bouncing.
5. **Press up** and slightly back, so the bar finishes over your shoulders again.
6. **Breathe out** near the top, then breathe in for the next rep.
7. **Rerack.** Finish the last rep with straight arms, then move the bar back into the hooks before you lower it.

## Safety: do these every time

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Power rack available | Set the safety bars just below your chest height when lying down |
| Plain flat bench, no safeties | Ask for a spot on any set that is hard, or use dumbbells |
| Asking for a spot | "Can you spot me for one set of 8? Help only if the bar stops." |
| Benching alone without safeties | Do not use collars, so plates can be tipped off if you get stuck |
| Pain in shoulder, elbow or wrist | Stop, and see a doctor or physiotherapist before you continue |

A spotter stands behind your head with hands close to the bar. Agree before the set whether they help with the unrack, and when they should step in.

## What weight to start with

An Olympic bar weighs 20 kg. Many Indian gyms also have lighter fixed bars of 10 to 15 kg, and dumbbells from 2.5 kg. Start lighter than you think.

| Weeks | Sets × reps | How to add weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | 3 × 10 | Same weight; learn the setup |
| 3–6 | 3 × 8–10 | Add 1–2.5 kg when all sets reach 10 clean reps |
| 7+ | 3–4 × 6–10 | Keep adding small amounts; repeat a week if reps drop |

The idea of adding small amounts over time is called progressive overload. Our [progressive overload guide](/guides/progressive-overload-explained/) and [sets and reps guide](/guides/how-many-sets-and-reps-to-build-muscle/) explain it in more detail.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elbows flared to 90° | Upper arms straight out to the sides | Tuck elbows to about 45–70°; bring the bar lower on your chest |
| Bouncing off the chest | Bar thumps the chest and shoots up | Lower for 2 seconds, light touch, then press |
| Hips lifting | Bum leaves the bench on hard reps | Keep feet flat and drive through the floor, not upward |
| Loose shoulders | Shoulder blades spread, shoulders roll forward | Squeeze them together before you unrack |
| Bar too high | Lowering toward your neck | Aim for the lower chest |
| Partial reps | Stopping halfway down | Lower the weight and use the full range |
| Uneven press | One side rises first | Lighter weight; watch a video of your set |

The quickest way to spot these is to film one set from the side and one from your feet. You can film a set in SpotAI and review it afterwards. Our guide to [checking your form with your phone camera](/guides/check-exercise-form-with-phone-camera/) covers where to place the phone.

## A good alternative: the dumbbell bench press

If the barbell feels awkward, or there is no spotter, start with dumbbells. Each arm works on its own, you can drop them safely to the sides, and it is easier to find a comfortable shoulder position. The setup is the same: five points of contact, shoulder blades back, and controlled reps.

If you have had a shoulder, elbow or wrist injury, ask a physiotherapist which pressing exercises suit you before starting.
