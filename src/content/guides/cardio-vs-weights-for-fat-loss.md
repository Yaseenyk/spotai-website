"Should I do cardio or weights to lose fat?" is one of the most common questions at any gym. The honest answer is that it is not either-or. Each does a different job, and what you eat decides most of the result.

## What each one does

| | Cardio | Weight training |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calories burned per session | Higher | Lower to moderate |
| Effect on muscle | Little; can lose some in a big deficit | Keeps or builds muscle |
| Effect on shape | You get smaller | You get smaller and firmer |
| Heart and lungs | Strong benefit | Some benefit |
| Easy to do daily | Yes (walking, cycling) | Needs rest days for the same muscles |

Fat loss happens when you use a little more energy than you eat, week after week. Exercise adds to the energy you use. Food controls what comes in.

## What the research found

The clearest trial on this question is STRRIDE AT/RT from Duke University, published in 2012. Overweight adults who had not been exercising did eight months of either cardio, weight training, or both.

- The **cardio group** and the **combined group** lost more fat and body weight than the weights-only group.
- The **weights group** and the **combined group** gained lean (muscle) mass. The cardio-only group did not.
- The **combined group** did the most total exercise and was the only one that both lost fat and gained muscle.

So cardio was better for the number on the scale, and weights were better for keeping muscle. Doing both gave the best change in body shape.

The WHO also recommends both for adults: 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity (or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity) a week, plus muscle-strengthening work on at least two days.

## How many calories do common sessions burn?

Rough figures for a 70 kg person, worked out from the Compendium of Physical Activities. Your number will differ with your weight, effort and fitness.

| Activity | Time | Approx. calories |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brisk walk (about 5.5 km/h) | 30 minutes | 150 |
| Stationary cycle, moderate | 30 minutes | 245 |
| Running (about 10 km/h) | 30 minutes | 345 |
| Weight training, moderate | 45 minutes | 185 |
| Weight training, hard | 45 minutes | 315 |

Notice the gap is smaller than many people think, and it is easily wiped out by one extra plate of something fried. That is why food matters so much. Our guide to [calories in Indian food](/guides/calories-in-indian-food/) can help you see where your calories come from.

## A simple weekly plan

This works for most beginners and fits into four or five gym visits.

| Day | Session |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Weights: full body (40–50 minutes) |
| Tuesday | Cardio: 30 minutes cycle or brisk incline walk |
| Wednesday | Weights: full body |
| Thursday | Rest, or a 30–45 minute walk outside |
| Friday | Weights: full body |
| Saturday | Cardio: 30–40 minutes of your choice |
| Sunday | Rest |

Plus: aim for a daily walk. Steps add up and are easy to recover from.

If you only have three days, lift on all three and add 10 to 15 minutes of cardio at the end of each session, then walk on other days.

For the weight sessions, keep the plan simple and add a little weight or a rep over time. Our [progressive overload guide](/guides/progressive-overload-explained/) explains how.

## What to eat for fat loss

- **Eat a little less, not a lot less.** A small deficit is easier to keep and helps you hold on to muscle. Crash diets usually end in a rebound.
- **Keep protein high.** It helps you keep muscle while you lose fat. See [how much protein you need per day](/guides/how-much-protein-per-day-for-gym/).
- **Watch the extras.** Sugar in chai, fried snacks, sweets at work and second helpings of rice add up faster than any workout burns.
- **Do not "earn" food with cardio.** Many people eat back more than they burned.

## How to tell if it is working

The scale moves up and down with water, salt and food in your stomach. Look at several signs together:

- Waist measurement once every two weeks
- Weights going up, or the same weights feeling easier
- Photos once a month in the same light
- How your clothes fit

If your waist is going down and your lifts are steady or rising, you are losing fat and keeping muscle, even if the scale moves slowly.

If you have diabetes, a heart condition, or are on medication, check with your doctor before changing how much you eat or exercise.
