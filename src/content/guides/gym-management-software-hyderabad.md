Hyderabad has gyms of every size, from 40-member neighbourhood setups in Kukatpally to multi-floor clubs in Gachibowli. The software that suits one can be wrong for another. This guide helps you choose by what your gym actually needs, not by the longest feature list.

## Start with your problems, not features

Write down the three things that cost you the most time or money each week. For most local gyms they are:

1. **Attendance.** Who came today, and is the register up to date?
2. **Dues.** Who has not paid, and who needs a reminder this week?
3. **Dropouts.** Who has quietly stopped coming before their plan ends?

Any software you consider should make these three easier on day one. If it does not, extra features will not help. For more on the third one, see [how to stop gym members dropping out](/guides/how-to-reduce-gym-member-dropouts/).

## Do you need software at all?

Not always. A paper register or spreadsheet works well for a small gym if:

- someone updates it every day, and
- someone reads it every week to find unpaid and missing members.

If you want to start there, use our free [gym attendance register](/guides/gym-attendance-register/). Software is worth paying for when the register is not being read, when members cross a few hundred, or when you run more than one branch.

## What it costs in 2026

Prices vary a lot and change often. On the Techjockey marketplace in November 2026, listed starting prices for gym software included:

- **Monthly plans:** roughly ₹500 to ₹2,000 a month for basic tiers.
- **Yearly plans:** roughly ₹4,000 to ₹18,000 a year.
- **Price on request:** several vendors do not publish prices.

Custom-built systems are a different category. One Hyderabad developer quotes from about ₹1.5 lakh for a basic build. For most single-branch gyms, ready-made software is the better first step.

Watch for three things when comparing quotes:

- **Monthly vs yearly.** ₹999 a month is about ₹12,000 a year. Put every quote on the same basis.
- **Per user or per branch.** Some prices are per staff login or per branch.
- **Add-ons.** Biometric devices, SMS credits, WhatsApp messaging, a branded app and setup fees are often extra. Ask whether GST is included.

## Hyderabad-specific checks

- **Staff language.** Your front desk may be more comfortable in Telugu, Hindi or Urdu than English. Watch them use the software in the demo, not just the owner.
- **Cash and UPI.** Many members still pay in cash or by UPI to the owner's number. The software should let you mark these as paid in a few taps.
- **WhatsApp.** Members read WhatsApp, not email. Ask how reminders are sent and whether messages go from your number. For message wording, see our [gym fee reminder templates](/guides/gym-fee-reminder-message/).
- **Local support.** Ask if someone can visit to set up, and what support hours are. A vendor with an office in the city is useful if things go wrong.

## Questions to ask in every demo

1. Can I import my current member list from Excel?
2. Can I export all my data, including payment history, if I leave?
3. What exactly is included in the price, and what costs extra?
4. Where is member data stored, and who can see it?
5. What does the member see on their phone, if anything?
6. How long does it take to check in one member at the desk?

Member phone numbers, payment records and attendance are personal data. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 puts duties on businesses that collect it, so ask the vendor how they protect it and how you can delete a member's data on request.

## A two-week trial plan

1. **Day 1–2:** Import members, set up plans and staff logins.
2. **Day 3–9:** Check in every member, every visit. Mark every payment.
3. **Day 10:** Pull the list of unpaid members and those not seen in 7 days. Is it right?
4. **Day 14:** Ask your front-desk staff whether it saved them time or added work.
