The treadmill is the first machine many beginners walk to, and often the one they get stuck on. Thirty minutes at the same speed, staring at a wall, is why people stop going. Here is how to use it safely, then how to make the time go faster.

## Before you press start

- **Find the safety key.** It is a clip on a cord, usually red, that plugs into the console. Clip it to your waistband or shirt. If you slip or drift back, it pulls out and the belt stops.
- **Find the stop button.** Usually a large red button in the middle of the console.
- **Shoes and clothes.** Proper trainers, laces tied. No slippers, no loose dupatta or long ends that could catch.
- **Water bottle and towel** in the holders, not on the belt deck.

## Your first time, step by step

1. **Stand on the side rails**, one foot on each, not on the belt.
2. **Press start** or quick start. The belt begins slowly, usually around 1 km/h.
3. **Raise the speed to 3–4 km/h** while still on the rails.
4. **Hold the handrails and step on**, walking with the belt.
5. **Let go** once you feel steady and walk normally for 2–3 minutes.
6. **Increase the speed** in small steps until you reach a brisk walk.
7. **To finish**, lower the speed to a slow walk for 2–3 minutes, then press stop and wait for the belt to stop fully before you step off.

Most gym treadmills in India show speed in km/h. If yours shows mph, 1 mph is about 1.6 km/h.

## A speed guide in km/h

| Speed | What it feels like for most adults |
| --- | --- |
| 3–4 km/h | Easy walk, warm-up |
| 5–6.5 km/h | Brisk walk, can talk in short sentences |
| 7–8 km/h | Very fast walk or slow jog |
| 8–10 km/h | Comfortable to steady jog |
| 10+ km/h | Running |

These ranges vary with your height and fitness. Use the talk test: if you can say a sentence but not sing, you are working at a moderate level.

## Posture

- **Stand tall**, eyes forward, not down at your feet.
- **Stay near the front** of the belt, without kicking the motor cover.
- **Short, natural steps.** Over-striding pounds your heels.
- **Arms swing** at your sides; hold the rails only for balance when changing settings.

## Use incline before speed

If you are not ready to run, incline is the easiest way to make walking harder. Walking at 5 km/h on an 8–10% incline can feel as hard as a jog for many people, with less pounding on your joints. Start at 2–3% and add a step each week.

## Four workouts that are not boring

Each takes 20–30 minutes. Always start and end with 3–5 minutes of easy walking.

### 1. The incline walk (25 min)

Brisk walking speed throughout. Raise the incline by 1% every 2 minutes until you reach 8–10%, hold for 4 minutes, then come back down 1% every minute.

### 2. Walk intervals (20 min)

Alternate 1 minute of very fast walking (6.5–7 km/h) with 2 minutes at your normal brisk pace. Repeat 6 times.

### 3. The pyramid (24 min)

Every 3 minutes, raise the speed by 0.5 km/h for four rounds, then come back down the same way. Start at your brisk walking speed.

### 4. Walk-run (25 min)

For beginners moving towards running: 1 minute easy jog, 2 minutes brisk walk. Repeat 6–8 times. Each week, add 30 seconds to the jog.

A plan on your phone, a podcast or a playlist that matches the intervals also helps. Watching the clock does not.

## How often

The WHO recommends adults get 150–300 minutes of moderate activity a week, such as brisk walking, plus muscle-strengthening on two or more days. A treadmill session on three to five days, alongside some weights, covers both. ## Treadmill etiquette at peak hours

Treadmills are the most fought-over machines between 6 and 8 am and in the evening rush.

- Follow your gym's time limit, often 20–30 minutes at peak hours.
- Wipe the handrails and console when you finish.
- Do not leave a towel on a treadmill to "book" it.

See [gym etiquette](/guides/gym-etiquette/) for the rest, or [the best time to go to the gym](/guides/best-time-to-go-to-the-gym/) if you would rather avoid the queue.

## When to stop

Slow down and stop if you feel dizzy, short of breath beyond what you expect, or have chest pain. If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, or joint problems, check with your doctor before starting a new cardio routine.
