In many Indian gyms, a new member pays, gets a quick tour, and is then left to the floor trainer, who is already looking after twenty people. Whether that member is still coming in three months often depends on what that trainer does in the first four weeks. This guide is for owners to share with their trainers.

## Why the first month matters

New members are building a habit from scratch. Research on habit formation found that a new daily behaviour took a median of about 66 days to feel automatic, with a wide range. That means for the first month, every visit still takes effort. Small things tip the balance: being greeted by name, knowing what to do next, not feeling lost.

A trainer cannot make someone come. A trainer can make coming in feel easy.

## The first session (30–45 minutes)

1. **Ask three questions.** What is your goal? Have you trained before? Any injuries, pain or medical conditions? If they mention a condition or ongoing pain, suggest they check with a doctor before starting.
2. **Agree days and time.** Three fixed days a week is a good default. Write them down.
3. **Give a written plan.** Five or six exercises, sets and reps, on paper or in an app. Our [beginner gym workout plan](/guides/gym-workout-plan-for-beginners/) is a good template.
4. **Teach, do not test.** Show each exercise, let them try with a light weight, correct one thing at a time.
5. **End on time and easy.** They should leave feeling they could have done more.

## What to teach first

| Pattern | Beginner exercise | The one cue that matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | Goblet squat or box squat | Knees follow the toes |
| Hinge | Dumbbell Romanian deadlift | Hips back, back flat |
| Push | Incline push-up or machine chest press | Body straight, full range |
| Pull | Lat pulldown or seated row | Pull with elbows, chest up |
| Cardio | Treadmill or cycle | Safety key, start slow |

These five cover a full-body plan. Machines are fine for week one; free weights can come in as confidence grows. This matches the ACSM's general advice for novices: two to three full-body sessions a week, moderate loads around 8–12 reps, and slow progression.

## Week by week

| Week | Trainer's focus | Time per visit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Greet by name, check form on 1–2 exercises, answer questions | 5–10 min |
| 2 | First check-in: "How does the plan feel?" Adjust one thing | 10 min |
| 3 | Add weight or a new exercise if form is good; show progress in their log | 5 min |
| 4 | Month review: visits, weights then vs now, goal for month two | 15 min |

### Week 1: be seen

Say hello by name on their second and third visit. Watch one set of their squats or rows and give one cue. That is enough. It costs nothing, and it is often what makes a new member feel they belong.

### Week 2: the first check-in

Ask: too easy, too hard, or confusing? Change one thing. A beginner who sees their plan adjusted for them feels looked after.

### Week 3: show progress

Point to their log: "You started at 10 kg on the press, now you're at 15." Early strength gains come fast, and beginners rarely notice them on their own.

### Week 4: the month review

Count their visits. Compare their weights with week one. Set one goal for month two. If they want more help, this is the natural moment to mention personal training, not on day one.

## When a new member goes missing

If someone in their first two months has not come in for 7 days, the trainer sends a short, personal message. For example:

> "Hi Ravi, haven't seen you since last Tuesday. All okay? Your Thursday 7 am slot is free, shall I keep it for you?"

- Use their name and their usual time.
- Offer something useful: a slot, a shorter plan, a training partner.
- Do not send discounts or guilt.

Our guide on [stopping members from dropping out](/guides/how-to-reduce-gym-member-dropouts/) has more on this.

## Running the floor at peak hours

Between 6 and 8 am and in the evening rush, one trainer cannot coach everyone. Prioritise:

1. **Members in their first two weeks.** They need you most.
2. **Anyone using heavy weights with poor form.** Safety first.
3. **Regulars** can usually wait or be helped between sets.

Suggest quieter times to new members who have flexible schedules. A beginner on an empty floor learns faster and feels less watched.

## Handover between shifts

When morning and evening trainers both see a member, keep a simple shared note: plan given, weights, injuries mentioned, last check-in. A member should not have to explain their knee twice.

## Mistakes trainers make

- **Destroying them on day one** to show how hard the gym is.
- **Copying their own programme** onto a beginner.
- **Only helping members who buy PT.** The free floor members are next year's PT clients.
- **Talking about diet as if they are a doctor.** General advice is fine; specific conditions go to a doctor or dietitian.
- **Not noticing absences** until the renewal date has passed.

## What owners should check

Each week, look at three numbers for members who joined in the last 30 days: how many came at least twice a week, how many have not come in 7 days, and whether a trainer has followed up.
