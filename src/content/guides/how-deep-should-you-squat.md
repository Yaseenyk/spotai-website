"Go lower" is one of the most common things trainers say during a squat. Most beginners stop higher than they think, because the bottom of the squat is where it gets hard and where it is hardest to see yourself.

## What good depth looks like

From the side, at the bottom of the rep:

- The **crease of your hip** is level with, or a little below, the **top of your knee**.
- Your **heels stay on the floor**.
- Your **back stays in a strong, neutral position**, without rounding at the bottom.

That is often called squatting to **parallel**. Squatting lower is fine if you can keep the same control. Squatting higher (a "half squat") still trains your legs, but less through the full range, and it is easy to keep cutting it shorter as the weight goes up.

## Why people stop too high

- **Too much weight.** The fastest way to lose depth is to load more than you can control.
- **Heels lifting.** Tight ankles or weight on the toes makes the bottom position feel unstable.
- **Stance.** Feet too narrow, or toes pointing straight ahead, can block the hips.
- **Fear of the bottom.** It feels like you might not get back up. Practice fixes that.

## Three drills to squat deeper

### 1. Box squat to a target

Set a bench or box so that touching it puts you at parallel. Sit back and down until you lightly touch it, then stand up. Lower the box over a few weeks as you improve. Do 3 sets of 8.

### 2. Goblet squat

Hold one dumbbell at your chest. The weight in front helps you stay upright and sit lower. Pause for a second at the bottom and use your elbows to gently push your knees out. Do 3 sets of 10.

### 3. Pause squat

With a light weight, squat down, hold the bottom for two seconds, then stand. The pause teaches you that the bottom is a position you can control. Do 3 sets of 6.

## A quick ankle check

Kneel facing a wall with your front foot about one hand's length from it. Keep the heel down and try to touch the wall with your knee. If you cannot, ankle mobility may be limiting your depth. Spend a minute a day on this stretch and on slow, controlled bodyweight squats.

## How to check your depth

1. Prop your phone at hip height, side-on, about two metres away.
2. Film one set and pause the video at the bottom of each rep.
3. Compare the hip crease with the top of the knee.
