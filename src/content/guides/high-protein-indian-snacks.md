Snacks are an easy place to add protein. In many Indian homes, the 5 pm snack is chai with biscuits, namkeen or a samosa: plenty of calories, very little protein. Swapping that one snack can add 10 to 20 g of protein a day.

## What counts as a high-protein snack?

For this guide, a high-protein snack gives **about 10 g of protein or more in a normal portion**. For comparison, most people who train regularly aim for roughly 1.4 to 2 g of protein per kg of body weight a day, which is 85 to 120 g for someone who weighs 60 kg. See [how much protein you need per day](/guides/how-much-protein-per-day-for-gym/) for your number.

## High-protein Indian snacks, by protein per portion

Approximate values. Recipes, brands and portion sizes vary.

| Snack | Portion | Protein | Veg / egg / non-veg |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chicken tikka | 100 g cooked | about 25 g | Non-veg |
| Paneer tikka | 100 g paneer | about 18 g | Veg |
| Soya chunk chaat | 30 g dry chunks | about 15 g | Veg |
| Egg bhurji | 2 eggs | about 12 g | Egg |
| Boiled eggs | 2 eggs | about 12 g | Egg |
| Moong dal chilla | 2 chillas (50 g dry dal) | about 12 g | Veg |
| Besan chilla | 2 chillas (50 g besan) | about 11 g | Veg |
| Sprouts chaat | from 40 g dry moong | about 9 g | Veg |
| Sattu drink | 40 g sattu in water | about 8 g | Veg |
| Milk | 1 glass (250 ml) | about 8 g | Veg |
| Curd | 1 cup (200 g) | about 7 g | Veg |
| Roasted peanuts | 30 g (a small handful) | about 7 g | Veg |
| Roasted chana | 30 g | about 6 g | Veg |

The last few are not 10 g on their own, but they are cheap and easy to combine.

## Snacks that sound high-protein but are not

| Snack | Portion | Protein | Why it disappoints |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Makhana | 30 g bowl | about 3 g | Light and filling, but low in protein |
| Peanut chikki | 30 g piece | about 4 g | Half sugar or jaggery |
| Namkeen and bhujia | a handful | a few grams | Mostly fat, salt and calories |
| "Protein" cookies | 1–2 cookies | varies | Check the label; many give little protein per serving |
| Granola bars | 1 bar | usually under 5 g | Mostly oats and sugar |

These are fine as treats. They just will not move your protein total much.

## Easy combos for 15–20 g

| Combo | Protein (approx.) |
| --- | --- |
| 2 boiled eggs + 1 glass milk | 20 g |
| Soya chaat (30 g dry) + small bowl curd | 18–20 g |
| Besan chilla + 1 cup curd | 18 g |
| 30 g roasted chana + 30 g peanuts + 1 cup curd | 20 g |
| Paneer bhurji (75 g paneer) on 1 roti | 16–17 g |
| Sprouts chaat + 1 glass buttermilk | 12–14 g |

## By how much time you have

**Two minutes, no cooking**
- Roasted chana and peanuts
- Curd with a spoon of roasted chana on top
- Glass of milk
- Boiled eggs made in a batch on Sunday, kept in the fridge

**Ten minutes**
- Egg bhurji
- Soya chaat: soak chunks in hot water for 5 minutes, squeeze, mix with onion, tomato, lemon and chaat masala
- Paneer bhurji
- Besan or moong dal chilla (soak moong dal the night before)

**Carry to office or college**
- Roasted chana and peanuts in a small box
- Sattu powder with roasted jeera and salt, mixed with water when needed
- Boiled eggs
- Sprouts chaat in a closed box (eat within a few hours)

## Before and after the gym

- **1–2 hours before**: something light that sits well. A banana with a glass of milk, or curd with roasted chana. More ideas in [what to eat before the gym](/guides/pre-workout-meal-indian/).
- **After**: a snack with 15 to 20 g of protein, then your normal meal. Eggs and milk, soya chaat with curd, or a chilla with curd all work.

## Do you need protein bars or powder?

No. They can be convenient, but every snack above does the same job, often for less money. If you are thinking about powder, read [do you need whey protein](/guides/is-whey-protein-necessary/) first.

If you want to know whether your snacks are making a difference, log a normal week in SpotAI with Indian foods and see your daily protein. You may find one swap is enough.

If you have kidney disease, diabetes, or a food allergy, ask your doctor or a dietitian before making big changes to how much protein you eat.
