Most gym owners set prices by looking at the gym down the road and charging a little less. That can work, but it can also leave you full of members and still losing money. A better method takes about an hour with a calculator.

## Step 1: Work out your break-even fee

List your fixed monthly costs. Here is a hypothetical example for a mid-sized neighbourhood gym. Your numbers will be different.

| Cost | Per month |
| --- | --- |
| Rent | ₹1,20,000 |
| Salaries (trainers, front desk, cleaning) | ₹1,50,000 |
| Electricity and water | ₹40,000 |
| Equipment maintenance and repairs | ₹20,000 |
| Software, internet, marketing, other | ₹20,000 |
| **Total** | **₹3,50,000** |

Now divide by the number of **paying, active** members you can realistically have. Say you expect 250:

**₹3,50,000 ÷ 250 = ₹1,400 per member per month**

That ₹1,400 is your floor, before GST and before any profit or equipment replacement. If your average fee falls below it, you lose money however busy the floor looks.

Do not forget to set money aside for replacing machines and for loan repayments if you financed the equipment.

## Step 2: Check your local market

Visit or call five gyms within 3 to 4 km that are similar to yours. Note:

- monthly, quarterly and annual prices
- whether GST is included
- registration or admission fees
- what is included: trainers on the floor, classes, steam, parking, AC

Then decide where you sit. If your gym is newer, has better equipment or more trainer attention, you do not need to be the cheapest. For a sense of the range in one city, see our guide to [gym fees in Hyderabad](/guides/gym-fees-in-hyderabad/).

## Step 3: Build a plan ladder

Longer plans give you cash upfront and members a lower monthly rate. Keep the steps clear and simple.

| Plan | Example price | Per month | Discount vs monthly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly | ₹2,000 | ₹2,000 | — |
| Quarterly | ₹5,400 | ₹1,800 | 10% |
| Half-yearly | ₹9,600 | ₹1,600 | 20% |
| Annual | ₹16,800 | ₹1,400 | 30% |

In this hypothetical example, the annual plan lands exactly on the ₹1,400 break-even. Any deeper discount means annual members cost you money if most of them keep coming.

Be careful with very cheap annual plans sold in January. They bring in cash, but if many members stop coming in March, they also bring a lot of complaints, refund requests and bad reviews. Members who keep coming are what make a gym grow.

## Step 4: Handle GST clearly

Since 22 September 2025, GST on gym and fitness centre services is **5% without input tax credit** (it was 18% before). Without input tax credit, you cannot claim back GST paid on your own purchases against it.

You have two ways to show prices:

| Method | Board price | Member pays | You keep (before costs) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GST included | ₹2,100 | ₹2,100 | ₹2,000 |
| GST extra | ₹2,000 + GST | ₹2,100 | ₹2,000 |

The result is the same. Showing the GST-included price is clearer and avoids arguments at the counter. Check registration thresholds and filing rules with your chartered accountant.

## Step 5: Decide on extras

- **Registration fee.** Common in India, but a large one puts people off. Many gyms waive it as a joining or referral offer.
- **Personal training.** Price it separately and share a fair cut with the trainer.
- **Off-peak plans.** A cheaper plan for 11 am to 4 pm fills quiet hours without crowding the evening rush.
- **Couple, family and student plans.** A modest discount for joining together often brings in two members for less marketing effort.
- **Freeze rules.** Decide how many freeze days each plan gets, and put it in writing.

## Step 6: Raising prices fairly

Costs rise every year, so you will need to raise fees at some point.

1. **Raise for new members first.** Watch enquiries and conversions for a month.
2. **Give current members notice**, at least a month, on WhatsApp and at the desk.
3. **Explain the reason**: new equipment, a new trainer, higher electricity costs.
4. **Let members renew once at the old rate.** It rewards loyalty and softens the change.
5. **Keep it moderate.** Several small increases over the years are easier than one large jump.

Pricing only works if fees are collected. Track dues by plan and by date, and send reminders before the expiry date, not after.
