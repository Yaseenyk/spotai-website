Most gym rules are written on a board near the reception. The unwritten ones matter just as much. They decide whether the gym feels friendly or stressful, especially at 7 pm on a Monday when everyone wants the bench press.

## The core rules

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Put weights and plates back where they belong | The next person should not have to unload your 100 kg |
| Wipe the bench or pad after use | Sweat on a bench is the most common complaint in any gym |
| Use clean indoor shoes | Outdoor shoes bring in dust and mud, especially in the monsoon |
| Keep music in your earphones | Not everyone shares your taste, and the gym may already play music |
| Share at busy times | Let others work in between your sets |
| Respect personal space | Do not stand right behind or in front of someone mid-set |
| No unasked advice | Most people did not come to be coached by strangers |
| Do not film others | Their privacy matters as much as yours |

## Sharing equipment at peak hours

In most Indian cities, gyms fill up between about 6 and 9 in the morning and 6 and 9 in the evening. That is when sharing matters most.

- **Ask "How many sets left?"** before waiting next to someone. It is polite and tells you whether to wait.
- **Work in.** If someone is resting, ask to do your set between theirs. Change the weight back afterwards.
- **Do not hold three things at once.** Saving a bench, a pair of dumbbells and a cable machine for a superset is fine in an empty gym, not at 7 pm.
- **Use the right station.** Curls belong in the dumbbell area, not in the squat rack while people wait to squat.
- **Keep rest times reasonable.** Get off the machine between sets if others are waiting.

If you can choose, training at a quieter time makes everything easier. See [the best time to go to the gym](/guides/best-time-to-go-to-the-gym/).

## Hygiene, in Indian weather

Gyms in India are warm for much of the year, and many rely on fans or an AC that struggles in May.

- **Carry a small towel** and put it on benches before you lie down.
- **Use deodorant**, and wash gym clothes after every session. Synthetic t-shirts hold smells.
- **Bring indoor shoes in a bag.** Change at the entrance if the gym asks you to.
- **Do not spit**, including into the drinking-water area or the dustbin.
- **Wipe the cardio machines too.** Treadmill handles and cycle seats get just as sweaty.
- **Use the dustbin** for tissues, bottle caps and supplement sachets.

## Phones, filming and music

- **Calls:** step away from the training floor. Long video calls on a bench are a common irritation.
- **Filming your own set** to check your form is fine and useful. Place the phone where nobody else is in the shot, and do not block a walkway. In SpotAI you can film a set and review it later, but the same rule applies: only you in the frame.
- **Never film other members** without asking, and never in changing rooms.
- **Shared speakers:** if your gym has one, ask staff before changing the music. Do not play your own speaker.

## Respect and space

- **Do not stare.** Look at your own reflection, not at other people's.
- **No comments on bodies**, appearance or weight, even if meant as praise.
- **Give everyone space**, and be especially mindful in mixed gyms. Many women train in free-weight areas, and they should not have to deal with hovering, unasked tips or attempts to chat.
- **Grunting** on a heavy lift is normal. Shouting on every rep is not.
- **Drop weights gently.** Dropping dumbbells from the top damages the floor and the dumbbells, and startles people.

## Trainers and personal clients

- A trainer with a paying client is working. Wait until they finish before asking questions.
- Floor trainers are usually there to help everyone: ask them how to adjust a machine, or to watch a set.
- Do not borrow the equipment a trainer has set up for a client without asking.

## When someone else breaks the rules

- **Small things**, like plates left on a bar: unload it yourself or ask nicely, "Are you still using this?"
- **Repeated issues**, like someone always hogging a station at peak time: tell the front desk instead of arguing.
- **Safety issues**, like someone stuck under a bar: help, then let staff know.

## If you are new

Nobody expects you to know everything on day one. Ask the front desk about the rules, watch how others share equipment for a week, and you will pick it up.
