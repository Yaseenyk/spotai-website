Most Indian gyms sell 3, 6 and 12-month plans paid upfront. Then life happens: a transfer, a wedding in the family, board exams, a surgery, a two-month trip home. Without a clear freeze policy, the member either loses money and leaves angry, or argues at the desk. A simple written policy avoids both.

## Why a freeze is good for your gym

It can feel like giving away days. Look at it the other way:

- A member who freezes **has told you they plan to come back**. A member who just stops coming has not.
- The freeze has a **return date**, so you know when to follow up.
- A fair freeze is something members **mention to friends**. An unfair one is something they mention online.

The cost to you is a few weeks added to the end of a plan the member has already paid for.

## A policy by plan length

Keep limits simple and tied to what the member paid for.

| Plan | Freeze allowed | Times per plan | Minimum per freeze |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly | None (do not renew instead) | – | – |
| 3 months | Up to 15 days | Once | 7 days |
| 6 months | Up to 30 days | Up to twice | 7 days |
| 12 months | Up to 60 days | Up to three times | 7 days |
| Medical (any prepaid plan) | Longer, with a doctor's note | Case by case | 14 days |

These are starting points, not a rule. Choose numbers you are comfortable with, then apply them to everyone. The minimum stops people freezing for two days every time they skip a weekend.

## Reasons and proof

You do not need a reason for a standard freeze within the limits. For anything beyond the limits, ask for something simple:

- **Medical:** a doctor's note or discharge summary. Do not ask for more detail than you need, and do not keep copies longer than necessary.
- **Transfer or travel:** a short message is usually enough.
- **Pregnancy:** allow a longer freeze, and do not ask for documents beyond confirmation.

## Fees

- **First freeze:** free on 6 and 12-month plans is a good default. It builds goodwill.
- **Extra freezes or very long ones:** a small admin fee is fair if it is written into the policy before the member pays.
- **Never** add fees that were not in the original terms. That is how disputes start.

## Sample policy wording

You can adapt this for your membership form:

> Members on 3, 6 or 12-month plans may freeze their membership for up to 15, 30 or 60 days respectively. Each freeze must be at least 7 days and requested at the front desk or by WhatsApp at least one day before it starts. Frozen days are added to the end date of the plan. Medical freezes beyond these limits are allowed with a doctor's note. Freezes cannot be applied to past dates. Monthly plans cannot be frozen.

Have the member sign or acknowledge it at joining, and give them a copy.

## Running a freeze, step by step

1. **Record it** with start date, end date and the new plan end date, in the member's record or your register.
2. **Confirm in writing.** A WhatsApp message with all three dates is enough.
3. **Two days before the freeze ends**, send a short message: "Your break ends on Monday. See you at your usual 7 am slot?"
4. **On the return day**, check whether they came in.

The "no backdating" line in the policy matters. If members can freeze retroactively, you will get requests to cover every month they skipped.

## The return is the important part

A member coming back from a month off is at high risk of dropping out. They are weaker than they were, the gym feels unfamiliar, and the old routine is gone.

- Have a trainer greet them and **cut their old weights by about a third** for the first two weeks.
- Give them a short plan for the first week back.
- If they have **not come in within a week** of the freeze ending, message them personally.

## Avoiding disputes

Most freeze disputes come from promises made at the desk that were never written down. Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, members who feel a service was not provided as promised can complain through the National Consumer Helpline or a consumer commission. You avoid this by being clear:

- Put the policy in writing at sign-up.
- Confirm every freeze in writing.
- Apply the same rules to everyone.
- If a case is unusual, decide it, write down what you agreed, and send it to the member.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For specific questions about your contracts, speak to a lawyer.
