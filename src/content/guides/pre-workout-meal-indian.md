What you eat before the gym affects how you feel in the session: whether you have energy for the last sets or feel heavy and slow. You do not need special products. Ordinary Indian food works well if you get the timing right.

## The simple rule

- **More time before training, bigger meal.** Two to three hours gives your body time to digest a normal plate.
- **Less time, smaller and simpler.** Within an hour, choose a light snack that is mostly carbohydrate.
- **Carbohydrates are the main fuel** for lifting and cardio. Some protein helps too.
- **Go easy on fat, fibre and spice right before** training. They take longer to digest.

Sports nutrition guidelines suggest eating carbohydrate-rich food in the one to four hours before exercise, with smaller amounts the closer you get to the session (ACSM, Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics and Dietitians of Canada, 2016).

## What to eat, by time before training

| Time before gym | What to eat | Indian options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2–3 hours | A normal meal: carbs + protein, moderate fat | 2 rotis with dal and sabzi; rice, dal and curd; 3 idlis with sambar; vegetable upma with a glass of milk; egg bhurji with 2 rotis |
| 1–1.5 hours | A light meal or large snack | A plate of poha with peanuts; 2 idlis; a besan chilla; bread with peanut butter; curd with banana |
| 30–60 minutes | A small, quick snack | 1 banana; 2–3 dates; a slice of toast with honey; a small bowl of curd with fruit |
| Less than 30 minutes | Very little, or just water | A few sips of water; half a banana if you feel hungry |

Portions depend on your size and goal. A 50 kg person and an 85 kg person do not need the same plate.

## If you train early in the morning

Many people go to the gym at 6 am, before work or college. You have three options:

1. **Train fasted.** Have water and start. Many people feel fine for a 45 to 60 minute session. Eat a proper breakfast afterwards.
2. **Have a quick snack** on the way: a banana, a few dates, or a glass of milk.
3. **Eat something small the night before** if you train very early and feel weak: a later dinner with enough carbohydrate helps.

If you feel dizzy or light-headed during a fasted session, stop, sit down, and eat something before your next workout. If this happens often, or you have diabetes or low blood pressure, check with your doctor.

## If you train after office

Evening trainees often have lunch at 1 pm and reach the gym at 7 pm, six hours later. That gap is why many people feel flat in evening sessions. Add a snack around 4 or 5 pm:

- Poha or upma
- A sprouts chaat
- A banana and a handful of roasted chana
- A sandwich with paneer or egg
- Curd with fruit

Then have dinner after training. This also stops you from arriving home very hungry and overeating.

## Vegetarian, egg and non-veg options

| Diet | 2–3 hours before | 30–60 minutes before |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vegetarian | Roti, dal, sabzi; idli sambar; paneer paratha (small, less oil) | Banana, dates, curd with fruit |
| Eggs | 2 eggs with 2 toast or rotis | Banana, a boiled egg if you tolerate it |
| Non-veg | Rice with a small portion of chicken or fish curry, less oil | Banana or toast |

## What to avoid right before training

- **Fried food:** samosa, vada, pakoda, puri. They are heavy and slow to digest.
- **Rich gravies** with a lot of oil or cream.
- **Very large meals** within an hour of training.
- **Lots of raw salad, rajma or chana** right before. High-fibre food can cause gas and discomfort during exercise.
- **Sugary energy drinks.** A banana does the same job for less.

## Water

Drink water through the day, not just at the gym. In the Indian summer, or in a gym without good ventilation, you lose a lot of water as sweat. A glass of water an hour or two before training and small sips during the session are a good habit.

## On fasting days

If you fast for Navratri, Ramzan or other occasions, keep training lighter and shorter. Schedule sessions after you have eaten where possible, for example after iftar or after your evening meal. If you have a medical condition, ask your doctor how fasting and exercise fit together for you.
