A good warm-up is short and specific. It gets your body temperature up, takes your joints through the moves you are about to do, and lets you practise the first lift with a light weight. It should leave you ready to train, not tired.

## The three parts of a warm-up

| Part | Time | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Easy cardio | 3–5 minutes | Raises heart rate and body temperature |
| 2. Joint and movement prep | 3–5 minutes | Moves hips, shoulders and spine through the range you will use |
| 3. Warm-up sets | 3–6 minutes | Practises the first lift and builds up to your working weight |

The American College of Sports Medicine suggests 5 to 10 minutes of light-to-moderate activity before exercise. The warm-up sets come on top of that, but they also count as practice, so they are time well spent.

## A 10-minute warm-up you can use for any session

**Easy cardio (4 minutes)**
- Cycle, cross-trainer, rowing machine or a brisk treadmill walk. Aim for "breathing a bit harder, can still talk".
- No machine free at 7 pm? Do 2 rounds of 20 jumping jacks and 20 marching steps on the spot.

**Movement prep (4–5 minutes)**
- 10 arm circles forwards and backwards
- 10 band pull-aparts, or 10 slow arm crosses with no band
- 10 leg swings front to back, each leg
- 10 bodyweight squats, slow
- 8 walking lunges, each leg
- 8 cat-cow movements on the floor, or 8 standing hip hinges with hands on hips

Then go straight to your first exercise.

## Warm-up sets: the part most people skip

Warm-up sets are lighter sets of the first exercise of the day. You do fewer reps as the weight goes up, so you are not tired when you reach your real sets.

Here is an example for someone whose working sets on the squat are 60 kg for 8 reps:

| Set | Weight | Reps | Rest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Empty bar | 10 | 30 seconds |
| 2 | About 50% (30 kg) | 6 | 45 seconds |
| 3 | About 70% (42.5 kg) | 4 | 60 seconds |
| 4 | About 85% (50 kg) | 2 | 90 seconds |
| Working sets | 60 kg | 8 | As planned |

If your working weight is light, two warm-up sets are enough. If it is heavy, you may want four or five. Use each warm-up set to check one thing: depth, foot position, bar path, breathing.

For the second and third exercises, one light set is usually enough if they train the same muscles. A new pattern, such as a lat pulldown after squats, gets one or two light sets.

## What about stretching before you lift?

Long static stretches (holding one position for a minute or more) right before lifting can slightly reduce strength and power for a short time. Short holds of under about 30 seconds per muscle make little difference either way, according to a large review by Behm and colleagues (2016).

In practice:
- **Before lifting**: use movement, such as leg swings, lunges and arm circles. If a muscle feels tight, a short hold is fine.
- **After lifting**: this is the better time for longer, slow stretches, held for 20 to 30 seconds each.

## Adjust the warm-up to the day

| Session | Add to the routine above |
| --- | --- |
| Legs | Extra bodyweight squats and glute bridges; 3–4 ramp sets on your first squat or leg press |
| Chest and shoulders (push) | Band pull-aparts, wall slides, 10 slow push-ups against a bench; 2–3 ramp sets on your first press |
| Back (pull) | Dead hangs of 10–20 seconds, light lat pulldowns for 12–15 reps |
| Cardio day | Start your cardio slowly for 5 minutes, then build the pace. That is your warm-up. |

If you are squatting, a few slow bodyweight squats to your full depth are a good test. You can read more on what depth to aim for in [how deep should you squat](/guides/how-deep-should-you-squat/), and a light [goblet squat](/guides/goblet-squat-for-beginners/) makes a good last warm-up drill.

## Short on time?

If you only have 45 minutes, do not skip the warm-up. Cut it to this:

1. 2 minutes of brisk walking or jumping jacks
2. 10 bodyweight squats, 10 arm circles each way, 10 leg swings each leg
3. Two warm-up sets of your first exercise

That takes about 5 minutes. Walking to the gym at a brisk pace can also cover the first part.

## Common mistakes

- **Spending 20 minutes on the treadmill first.** That uses energy you need for lifting. Five minutes is plenty.
- **Jumping straight to your working weight.** The first set then feels heavy, and your form tends to be worse.
- **Doing hard exercises as a warm-up.** Burpees, heavy kettlebell swings or balance tricks belong in the workout, not before it.
- **Treating warm-up sets as work.** Stop each warm-up set well short of tiring.

If you want to check that your form in the warm-up sets looks the same as in your working sets, film one of each from the side. In SpotAI you can film any set and review it later.

If you have pain, a past injury or a heart or joint condition, ask a doctor or physiotherapist how to warm up safely before you train.
