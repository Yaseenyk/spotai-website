The push-up looks basic, and that is the problem. Because it looks easy, beginners who cannot do one feel embarrassed and skip it. You do not need to start on the floor. You need to start at the right height and come down step by step.

## What a good push-up looks like

Use this checklist at every level, from wall to floor:

- **Hands** slightly wider than your shoulders, under or just outside your chest.
- **Body straight** from head to heels. No sagging hips, no bum in the air.
- **Core and glutes tight**, as if bracing for someone to poke your stomach.
- **Elbows** at about 45 degrees from your body, not flared straight out to the sides.
- **Full range:** chest comes to about a fist's height from your hands, then push back to straight arms.

A half rep on the floor is less useful than a full rep on an incline. Do not chase the floor too early.

## The incline ladder

The higher your hands, the easier the push-up. Gyms are full of things at different heights, which makes this simple.

| Step | Where your hands go | Rough difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Wall | Easiest |
| 2 | Smith machine bar or rack bar at chest height | Very easy |
| 3 | Bar at waist height | Easy |
| 4 | Flat bench or a sturdy table | Moderate |
| 5 | Aerobic step or low box | Hard |
| 6 | Floor | Full push-up |

A Smith machine or a barbell set in a rack is ideal because you can lower it one notch at a time. Make sure the bar is locked in place and cannot roll.

## Find your starting step

1. Warm up your shoulders with arm circles and a few easy wall push-ups.
2. Pick a step and do as many good reps as you can.
3. If you get **8 to 12** with good form, that is your starting step.
4. More than 12? Go one step lower. Fewer than 8? Go one step higher.

Note your step and reps. That is your baseline.

## A 6-week plan to ten push-ups

Train two or three times a week, with a rest day in between. Put push-ups after your warm-up, near the start of your session.

| Week | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 3 sets of 8–10 at your starting step, 60–90 seconds rest |
| 2 | 3 sets of 10–12 at the same step |
| 3 | Drop one step. 3 sets of as many good reps as you can, up to 10 |
| 4 | Same step, aim for 3 sets of 10–12. Add one set of slow negatives one step lower: take 3 seconds to lower, then reset |
| 5 | Drop another step. 3 sets of 8–10 |
| 6 | Test: floor push-ups, as many good reps as you can |

If you cannot reach the floor by week 6, that is fine. Keep moving down the ladder. Some people take longer, especially if they are heavier or have never trained the upper body. The process is the same.

This follows the general advice from the American College of Sports Medicine for beginners: around 8–12 reps per set, two to three sessions a week, and small steps up in difficulty as you adapt.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Hips sagging | Squeeze your glutes and brace, or move up one step |
| Bum in the air | Bring your hips down to line up with shoulders and heels |
| Elbows flared to 90 degrees | Tuck them to about 45 degrees |
| Head dropping to the floor | Look at a spot a little ahead of your hands |
| Half reps | Lower the chest fully, even if it means fewer reps or a higher step |

The easiest way to spot sagging hips is from the side. Film one set and pause at the bottom. You can do this on any phone, or record the set in SpotAI and watch it back straight away.

## Exercises that help

- **Plank:** 3 holds of 20–40 seconds, body in the same straight line.
- **Dumbbell or machine chest press:** builds the same muscles with a load you can adjust. Our guide on [bench press form](/guides/bench-press-form-for-beginners/) covers the barbell version.
- **Triceps pushdowns:** strong triceps help you lock out the top of the push-up.

Warm up before any of these; see [how to warm up before lifting](/guides/how-to-warm-up-before-the-gym/).

## After ten

Once you can do ten good floor push-ups, keep building: add a set, slow down the lowering phase, or put your feet on a low step. The same rule applies. Add difficulty only while every rep still looks like the checklist.

If you have shoulder, elbow or wrist pain that does not settle, stop and see a doctor or physiotherapist before you continue.
