If you are new to squatting, start here. The goblet squat needs one dumbbell, takes five minutes to learn, and builds the same pattern you will use later with a barbell. Most trainers use it as the first squat for beginners because it is hard to do badly.

## Why it works for beginners

- **The weight keeps you upright.** Holding a dumbbell at your chest acts as a counterweight, so you can sit down between your knees without tipping forward.
- **Easy to bail.** If a rep goes wrong, you just stand up or put the dumbbell down. No rack, no spotter.
- **Every gym has the equipment.** A single dumbbell is enough. Kettlebells work too, but many Indian gyms have only a few.
- **It shows your weak spots.** Tight ankles, knees falling in or a rounding back show up at light weights, where they are easy to fix.

## How to hold the dumbbell

Stand the dumbbell on its end. Cup the top plate with both palms, like holding a bowl, with your thumbs hooked around the handle. Hold it against your breastbone, elbows pointing down. Keep it touching your chest the whole time. If it drifts away, it will pull you forward.

## How to do a goblet squat

1. **Stance.** Feet a little wider than your hips, toes turned out slightly, about 15 to 30 degrees.
2. **Brace.** Take a breath into your belly and tighten your stomach as if someone were about to poke it.
3. **Sit down.** Bend your hips and knees together. Push your knees out over your toes as you go down.
4. **Depth.** Go down until your hip crease is at or a little below the top of your knee, as long as your heels stay flat and your back stays neutral. Your elbows may brush the inside of your knees.
5. **Stand up.** Push the floor away through your whole foot. Keep your chest up and the dumbbell close.
6. **Breathe.** Breathe out at the top, take a new breath, then start the next rep.

## Common mistakes and quick fixes

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knees caving in | Knees fall towards each other on the way up | Lighter weight, think "knees out" and push through the outside of your feet |
| Heels lifting | Weight shifts to the toes at the bottom | Widen your stance a little, or put a 2.5 kg plate under each heel for now |
| Chest dropping | Dumbbell moves away from the chest | Keep elbows down and the weight touching your body |
| Stopping too high | Quarter squats | Squat to a bench or box set at the right height, then remove it |
| Rushing | Bouncing out of the bottom | Lower for about 2 seconds, pause briefly, then stand |

Knees falling in is one of the most common problems. Our [knees caving in guide](/guides/how-to-fix-knees-caving-in-squat/) has three drills for it.

## How heavy to start

Pick a dumbbell you can lift for 12 reps with every rep looking the same, and with two or three reps left in the tank. For many beginners that is 5 to 10 kg. Some will start with no weight at all, and that is fine.

Most dumbbell racks in Indian gyms go up in 2.5 kg steps, so progress in those jumps.

## A 6-week goblet squat plan

Do this two or three times a week, with at least one rest day between sessions.

| Week | Sets x reps | What to aim for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3 x 8 | Light. Learn the movement, film one set |
| 2 | 3 x 10 | Same weight, smoother reps |
| 3 | 3 x 12 | Same weight. If all sets feel easy, add 2.5 kg next week |
| 4 | 3 x 8 | Heavier dumbbell |
| 5 | 3 x 10 | Same weight |
| 6 | 3 x 12 | Same weight, then add 2.5 kg again |

This is the simple rule behind most beginner plans: once you can do all your sets at the top of the rep range with good form, add a little weight and drop back to the bottom of the range. Rest about 90 seconds between sets.

## Variations to try later

- **Heels raised.** Put small plates under your heels. This lets you stay more upright and works the thighs more. Useful if your ankles are stiff.
- **Pause goblet squat.** Hold the bottom for 2 seconds. Great for control and depth.
- **Box goblet squat.** Squat to a bench, touch lightly, stand up. Good for learning depth.
- **Kettlebell goblet squat.** Hold the kettlebell by the horns, bell down. Feels the same, just a different grip.

## When to move on to the barbell squat

Move on when the goblet squat stops being the limit, for example:

- You can do 3 x 12 with a dumbbell that is hard to hold or carry to your chest, often 20 kg or more.
- Your form stays the same on every rep, including the last.

Many people keep goblet squats as a warm-up for years. It is a good way to check your pattern before heavier sets.

## Check your form on video

You cannot see your own knees and hips while you squat. Prop your phone at hip height, about two metres away, and film one set from the side and one from the front. Look at your heels, knees and how far you go down. You can also film your set in SpotAI, and its coach flags knee cave, shallow depth and forward lean on each squat rep.

If you feel pain in your knees, hips or back while squatting, stop and get it checked by a doctor or physiotherapist before you continue.
