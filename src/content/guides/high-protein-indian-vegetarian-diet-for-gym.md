Protein helps your muscles recover and grow after training. For vegetarians in India the challenge is rarely finding protein foods: it is that everyday plates lean on roti and rice, with dal and sabzi in smaller amounts.

## How much protein you need

- India's recommended amount for adults is **0.83 g per kg of body weight a day** (ICMR-NIN, 2020). That covers basic needs.
- For people who train regularly, sports nutrition guidelines suggest **1.4 to 2 g per kg a day** (International Society of Sports Nutrition).

| Body weight | Basic need (0.83 g/kg) | Training (1.4–2 g/kg) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 kg | 42 g | 70–100 g |
| 60 kg | 50 g | 84–120 g |
| 70 kg | 58 g | 98–140 g |
| 80 kg | 66 g | 112–160 g |

If you are just starting, aim for the lower end of the training range and see how it fits into your meals before going higher.

## The best vegetarian protein foods in India

Approximate protein per common portion:

| Food | Portion | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soya chunks (dry weight) | 50 g | about 26 g |
| Paneer | 100 g | about 18–20 g |
| Matar paneer | 1 serving (277 g) | 18.3 g |
| Channa dal | 1 bowl (292 g) | 12.1 g |
| Palak paneer | 1 serving (296 g) | 11.9 g |
| Curd (dahi) | 1 cup (200 g) | about 7–8 g |
| Milk | 1 glass (250 ml) | about 8 g |
| Mixed dal | 1 bowl (298 g) | 7.5 g |
| Rajma curry | 1 serving (120 g) | 7.1 g |
| Roasted chana | 30 g | about 6 g |
| Peanuts | 30 g | about 7 g |

The cooked dishes come from the Indian Nutrient Databank (home-style recipes, one usual serving). Raw foods are typical values; brands and recipes vary.

## A sample day: about 90 g of protein

This is an example for a 60 kg beginner, not a prescription.

| Meal | What to eat | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breakfast | 2 moong dal chillas + 1 cup curd | about 20 g |
| Mid-morning | 1 glass milk | about 8 g |
| Lunch | 2 rotis, 1 bowl channa dal, salad, small bowl curd | about 22 g |
| Evening (after gym) | Soya chunk chaat (40 g dry) or roasted chana and peanuts | about 15–20 g |
| Dinner | 2 rotis + paneer curry, or rice + rajma with a side of paneer | about 20–25 g |

## Five habits that make it easy

1. **Protein first.** Fill a third of your plate with dal, paneer, chana or soya before adding roti or rice.
2. **Double the dal.** Two bowls instead of a small katori adds 7 to 12 g at lunch or dinner.
3. **Curd with two meals.** It is cheap, easy and goes with almost everything.
4. **Plan one soya or paneer dish a day.** It is the single biggest step up for most vegetarians.
5. **Have a protein snack after training.** Milk, roasted chana or a soya chaat work well.

## A note on balance

Protein matters, but so do total calories, vegetables, fruit and water, and sleep. If you have kidney problems, diabetes or another medical condition, check your protein target with your doctor or a registered dietitian before raising it.
