After a workout your muscles need protein to repair and carbohydrates to refill their energy stores. The good news: a normal Indian meal does both. You just need a bigger share of protein than most everyday plates have.

## What your post-workout meal needs

- **Protein: about 20 to 40 g.** The International Society of Sports Nutrition suggests this range per meal for people who train. Lighter people need the lower end.
- **Carbohydrates:** roti, rice, poha, potatoes or fruit. They refill the energy you used and help recovery.
- **Fluids:** water, buttermilk or lime water, especially after a sweaty session.
- **Vegetables:** for fibre and micronutrients. Not urgent, but part of a good plate.

## How soon should you eat?

Within an hour or two is fine for most people. A review of the research found that the "anabolic window" is wider than the 30 minutes often repeated in gyms, particularly when you have eaten a meal a few hours before training (Aragon and Schoenfeld, 2013).

What matters more:

- your **total protein for the day**, and
- spreading it across **three or four meals**.

If you trained fasted early in the morning, eat sooner rather than later, because your last meal was the night before.

## Indian post-workout meals with protein amounts

| Meal | Protein (approx.) |
| --- | --- |
| 2 katoris dal + rice + 1 bowl curd | 20–25 g |
| Paneer bhurji (100 g paneer) + 2 rotis | 24 g |
| Soya chunk pulao (40 g dry soya) + curd | 28–30 g |
| Rajma or chole (1.5 katori) + rice + curd | 20–23 g |
| Moong dal chilla (2) with paneer filling | 20–23 g |
| Egg bhurji (3 eggs) + 2 rotis | 24 g |
| Boiled eggs (2) + 4 egg whites + 2 toast | about 30 g |
| Chicken curry (150 g chicken) + rice or 2 rotis | 35–38 g |
| Grilled fish (150 g) + rice + sabzi | 30–33 g |
| Milk (300 ml) + banana + handful of peanuts | 17–18 g |

Protein values are estimates based on the Indian Food Composition Tables. Recipes and portions vary.

If you are vegetarian and struggle to reach these numbers, see our [high-protein Indian vegetarian diet](/guides/high-protein-indian-vegetarian-diet-for-gym/).

## Muscle gain vs fat loss

The protein stays the same. The rest of the plate changes.

| Goal | Plate |
| --- | --- |
| Muscle gain | Protein source + generous carbs (3 rotis or a full plate of rice) + vegetables + curd |
| Fat loss | Protein source + smaller carbs (1–2 rotis or half a plate of rice) + a big portion of vegetables |
| Maintenance | Protein source + your usual carbs + vegetables |

Say you weigh 70 kg and want to lose fat. Paneer bhurji with one roti, a big bowl of sabzi and a glass of buttermilk gives you about 25 g of protein without heavy calories. For muscle gain, add two more rotis or a bowl of rice.

## Morning, evening and late-night trainees

**Morning (6–8 am):** Your post-workout meal is breakfast. Make it protein-rich: eggs with toast, a besan or moong chilla with curd, or poha with a glass of milk and a boiled egg. Breakfast is usually the lowest-protein meal in Indian homes, so this is a good place to fix it.

**Evening (5–7 pm):** If dinner is two hours away, have a small snack after the gym (milk, roasted chana, a banana with peanuts), then a full dinner.

**Late night (8–10 pm):** Make dinner your post-workout meal. Keep it simple and not too heavy, so it does not disturb your sleep.

## Three common myths

1. **"You must drink a shake within 30 minutes."** Protein powder is a convenience. Food works just as well if it gives you enough protein.
2. **"No carbs after the gym if you want to lose weight."** Carbs after training are useful. Total calories for the day decide fat loss.
3. **"Only eggs and chicken build muscle."** Paneer, soya, dal, curd and milk work too. Vegetarians just need to plan bigger portions.

What you eat before training affects how you train. What you eat after, and across the whole day, affects how you recover.
