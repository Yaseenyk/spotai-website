Many Indian gyms still run on a paper register at the desk. It works until the evening rush, when ten people arrive at once, half of them skip the book, and nobody reads it again. QR check-in records every visit in a second and turns attendance into something you can act on.

## Two ways QR check-in works

| Model | How it works | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gym code at the entrance | One QR code on the wall or desk. Members scan it with the gym's app as they walk in. | Busy entrances, no extra staff work |
| Member's personal code | Each member has a code in their app. Staff scan it at the desk with a phone or tablet. | Gyms where the desk greets everyone anyway |

Some systems use a code on a screen that changes every few minutes, so a photo of it cannot be reused from home. Others add a location check so a scan only counts near the gym. A simple printed code is enough for most neighbourhood gyms.

## How QR compares with other methods

| Method | Hardware | Speed at rush hour | Data you get | Weak spots |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper register | None | Slow, queues form | Hard to read or total | Skipped entries, lost pages |
| Excel at the desk | Computer | Depends on staff | Good if kept up | Needs someone typing every visit |
| QR check-in | A printed code, members' phones | Fast | Visits by member, day and hour | Needs a desk fallback |
| RFID card | Reader, cards | Fast | Good | Cards get lost or lent |
| Biometric | Scanner device | Fast once enrolled | Good | Device cost, enrolment, consent for sensitive data |

## Setting it up in one week

**Day 1: choose the model.** Most gyms start with one code at the entrance plus a desk option.

**Day 2: print and place the code.**
- Print it at least A5 size so phones read it from a step away.
- Place it at chest height next to the door or on the desk, with good light.
- Add one line: "Scan to check in."

**Day 3: tell members.** A short message to all members and a sign at the desk. Ask trainers to remind people for the first two weeks.

**Day 4–7: watch the entrance at peak time.** See who scans, who forgets and where the queue forms. Move the code if people walk past it.

For the first month, have the desk ask "Checked in?" as people walk in. After that it becomes a habit.

## Handling the awkward cases

- **No smartphone, or phone left at home.** Staff check the member in by name at the desk. It should take seconds.
- **Weak mobile signal at the entrance.** Test it. If the signal is poor, put the code where Wi-Fi reaches or let the desk scan.
- **Older members.** A trainer helps them scan the first two or three times. Most are comfortable after that.
- **Shared codes.** A wall code records visits but does not stop someone lending their login. If that worries you, use personal codes scanned at the desk, or have staff glance at the screen during the rush.
- **Guests and day passes.** Check them in at the desk by name so they still show up in your numbers.

## What to do with check-in data

The record is only useful if someone reads it. Spend 15 minutes a week on this:

| Look at | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Members not seen in 7 days | A short personal message from a trainer |
| Members not seen in 14 days, plan ending this month | Call before the renewal date, not after |
| New members' visits in their first month | Fewer than 2 a week means they need help |
| Visits by hour | Add a trainer at peak hours, move classes to quiet ones |
| Visits by day | Plan maintenance and deep cleaning on the quietest day |

Members who stop coming rarely announce it. They just go quiet, then do not renew. Our guide on [stopping member dropouts](/guides/how-to-reduce-gym-member-dropouts/) explains what to do once you can see who has gone quiet.

## Privacy basics

Check-in data is personal data: names, phone numbers and when someone visits.

- **Tell members what you record** and why, for example attendance and reminders.
- **Limit access.** Not every staff member needs to see every member's history.
- **Do not share** visit times with anyone outside the gym.
- **Be extra careful with biometrics.** Fingerprints and face data need clear consent and secure storage.

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 sets rules on consent and on how personal data is used. Its rules are being brought in step by step, so check the current position or ask your CA or lawyer if you are unsure.

## A note on cost

A printed QR code costs nothing. The cost is in the app or software behind it, which varies a lot, so compare what the price includes: check-ins, member lists, reminders and reports.
