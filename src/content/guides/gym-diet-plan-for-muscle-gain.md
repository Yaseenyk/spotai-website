A good gym diet plan is not a secret list of foods. It is enough energy, enough protein and a pattern you can keep for months. This guide is a **gym diet plan for beginners** with a **7-day diet chart** you can adapt, whether you eat vegetarian or non-vegetarian.

## The three numbers that matter

1. **Energy.** To build muscle you need a little more than you burn. Beginners often gain muscle even at close to maintenance, so there is no need to force-feed.
2. **Protein.** About 1.4 to 2 g per kg of body weight a day for people who train regularly (International Society of Sports Nutrition, 2017). A large review found little added benefit above about 1.6 g per kg a day on average (Morton et al., 2018). India's basic recommendation for adults is 0.83 g per kg (ICMR-NIN, 2020), which covers needs but is not a muscle-building target.
3. **Consistency.** Eating this way for 12 weeks beats a perfect week followed by a bad month.

| Body weight | Gym target (1.6 g/kg) | Upper range (2 g/kg) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 kg | 80 g | 100 g |
| 60 kg | 96 g | 120 g |
| 70 kg | 112 g | 140 g |
| 80 kg | 128 g | 160 g |

If you currently eat 40 to 50 g a day, add 15 to 20 g every couple of weeks instead of jumping to the target.

## How to build each meal

- **Protein:** dal, paneer, tofu, eggs, chicken, fish, curd, soya chunks, chana, rajma.
- **Carbohydrate:** rice, roti, poha, oats, potato, banana. This fuels your training.
- **Vegetables and some fat:** sabzi, salad, a teaspoon or two of oil or ghee, nuts.

Spread protein over the day, about 20 to 40 g per meal every three to four hours. Breakfast is usually where Indian diets lose the most protein, so fix that first.

## A 7-day gym diet chart

Examples only. Change the dishes to what your family cooks and keep the pattern.

| Day | Breakfast | Lunch | Evening / after gym | Dinner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | 3-egg omelette, 2 toast, milk | 2 rotis, chicken curry, dal, salad | Banana and roasted chana | Rice, fish curry, curd |
| Tue | Paneer paratha (2), curd | Rice, rajma, sabzi, salad | Milk with peanuts | 2 rotis, dal, paneer bhurji |
| Wed | Moong dal chilla (3), curd | 2 rotis, egg curry, salad | Sprouts chaat | Rice, dal, sabzi, curd |
| Thu | Oats with milk, banana, almonds | Rice, chicken or chole, salad | Boiled eggs or roasted chana | 2 rotis, paneer, sabzi |
| Fri | Poha with peanuts, 2 boiled eggs or curd | 2 rotis, dal, soya sabzi | Fruit and milk | Rice, fish or tofu curry, dal |
| Sat | Besan chilla (3), curd | Rice, chicken curry or chana, salad | Peanut and jaggery chikki (small) | 2 rotis, dal, sabzi |
| Sun | Idli (4) with sambar, 2 eggs or curd | Family lunch, plus a katori of dal | Milk or curd | Light: khichdi, curd, salad |

A glass of milk or a bowl of curd before bed is an easy way to add a little protein.

## Around training

You do not need a special pre- or post-workout meal. Eat a normal meal with carbohydrate and protein one to three hours before, and another within a few hours after. If you train early and have little time, a banana and a glass of milk are enough.

## Supplements

Food can cover your needs. Whey protein is a convenient way to reach a target if you struggle with food alone, but it is optional, and ICMR-NIN's 2024 guidelines advise against protein supplements. Creatine and other products are outside this guide.

## Training matters too

Diet builds muscle only if you give the muscles a reason to grow. Use a simple routine such as the [gym workout plan for beginners](/guides/gym-workout-plan-for-beginners/), and see the [weight loss diet plan](/guides/7-day-diet-plan-for-weight-loss/) if your first goal is losing fat instead.

If you have kidney disease, diabetes or another medical condition, are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing your protein or calories.
