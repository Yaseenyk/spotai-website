Walk into any Indian gym on a Monday evening and the squat rack has a queue while the leg press sits free. So which one should you be doing? The honest answer is that both work, they do slightly different jobs, and the choice depends on your goal and where you are starting from.

## Side by side

| | Squat | Leg press |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main muscles | Quads, glutes, adductors, plus back and core to stay upright | Quads mainly, glutes and adductors with a deeper range |
| Balance and coordination | High | Low; the machine guides you |
| Learning curve | Weeks to get right | One session |
| Back loading | Bar sits on your back | Back supported by the pad |
| Easy to push close to failure safely | Needs safety bars or a spotter | Yes, with the safety stops |
| Equipment | Rack and bar, or a dumbbell for goblet squats | A leg press machine |

## What the research found

Two small studies are often quoted on this question.

- **Strength.** In a 10-week study (Rossi et al., 2018), groups trained with squats only, leg press only, or both. All groups improved their leg press by a similar amount, about 30%. But on the squat test, the squat-only group improved by about 32%, the mixed group by 20%, and the leg-press-only group by only 8%. The squat built leg press strength; the leg press did much less for the squat.
- **Jumping.** In an 8-week study (Wirth et al., 2016), the squat group improved jump height by about 12%, while the leg press group barely changed.

Both studies were small and in young, healthy adults, so treat them as a guide, not the final word. The pattern they show makes sense: skills you need to balance and stabilise are best trained by doing them.

For building muscle size, both lifts can work well when you train hard enough with enough sets. The muscles grow from the effort, not from the name of the exercise.

## Who should pick which

| Your situation | Lean towards |
| --- | --- |
| Beginner wanting overall strength | Learn the squat, starting with a goblet squat |
| Goal is bigger legs, already squatting | Both: squat first, leg press for extra sets |
| Sport, running, or jumping | Squat |
| Back pain or a past back injury | Leg press, after clearance from a doctor or physiotherapist |
| Older member, balance is a concern | Leg press and supported squats to a box |
| Rack always busy | Goblet squats or leg press; do not skip leg day |

If you are new to squatting, a [goblet squat](/guides/goblet-squat-for-beginners/) is the easiest way in.

## How to do each safely

### Squat

- Feet about shoulder-width, toes slightly out.
- Sit down and back, keeping your knees in line with your toes.
- Reach the depth you can control, ideally hip crease level with or below the top of the knee. Our guide on [how deep to squat](/guides/how-deep-should-you-squat/) explains how to get there.
- Keep your chest up and your heels down.

### Leg press

- Sit with your back and hips flat against the pad. Feet about shoulder-width, in the middle of the platform.
- Release the safety handles and lower the sled until your knees reach about 90 degrees, or as low as you can go **without your lower back lifting off the pad**.
- Push through your whole foot. Stop just short of locking your knees at the top.
- Re-engage the safety handles before you get out.

Foot position changes the feel: higher on the platform puts more work on glutes and hamstrings, lower puts more on the quads. Start in the middle.

## Sample leg days

Do one of these once or twice a week, after a warm-up.

**Beginner (weeks 1–6)**

| Exercise | Sets x reps |
| --- | --- |
| Goblet squat | 3 x 10 |
| Leg press | 3 x 12 |
| Romanian deadlift (dumbbells) | 3 x 10 |
| Calf raises | 2 x 15 |

**Intermediate**

| Exercise | Sets x reps |
| --- | --- |
| Barbell back squat | 4 x 6–8 |
| Leg press | 3 x 10–12 |
| Walking lunges | 2 x 10 each leg |
| Leg curl | 3 x 12 |

Leave 2–3 reps in reserve on squats. You can take the leg press closer to failure because the safety stops are there.

## The bottom line

If you can only pick one, the squat gives you more for general strength and movement. If you can do both, use both. And if the rack is busy, the leg press is a far better choice than leaving.

Squat form is hard to judge from the inside. Film a set side-on, or use SpotAI's coach, which watches your squats through the phone camera and flags knee cave, shallow depth and forward lean on each rep. If any lift causes knee or back pain, stop and get it checked by a doctor or physiotherapist.
