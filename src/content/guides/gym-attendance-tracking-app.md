Most Indian gyms still start with a register at the front desk. It costs nothing and everyone understands it. The trouble starts when you want to use it: who has not been in for two weeks, which hours are packed, whether January's new members are still coming. A register cannot answer those questions without someone spending an evening on it.

## What you actually want from attendance data

Before choosing a method, be clear about what you will do with the numbers:

- **Today:** who came in, so trainers can greet people by name.
- **Who has stopped coming:** members not seen in 7 or 14 days, while you can still bring them back.
- **New members:** whether people who joined in the last 60 days are coming at least twice a week.
- **Busy hours:** when to put more trainers on the floor and when to suggest quieter slots.
- **Disputes:** proof of visits if a member questions a plan or a freeze.

If a method makes these hard to see, it does not matter how modern it is.

## Check-in methods compared

| Method | Setup | What goes wrong | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper register | A notebook | Skipped entries, unreadable names, no reports | Very small gyms, as a backup |
| Excel or Google Sheet at the desk | A laptop or tablet | Staff forget at rush hour | Gyms with a dedicated desk person |
| Biometric (fingerprint or face) | Scanner and software | Sweaty or worn fingerprints fail, privacy concerns, device cost | Larger gyms, 24-hour access |
| RFID card or band | Reader plus cards | Lost cards, members lending cards | Gyms with turnstiles |
| QR code at the entrance | A printed QR and an app | Members without the app, or with a dead phone | Most small and mid-size gyms |
| Desk check-in by name | An app on the desk phone or tablet | Queue at peak hours if it is the only option | Backup for QR, older members |

Most gyms do well with two methods: a quick self check-in (QR) for most members, and a desk check-in by name for anyone who forgets their phone. ## Switching from paper in two weeks

### Week 1: run both

- Put the QR code or scanner at the entrance, at eye level, next to the register.
- Desk staff help every member check in once. Most people need to be shown only once.
- Keep the register going so you do not lose any days.

### Week 2: move over

- Ask members who still sign the register to try the new method.
- Older members or people without smartphones get checked in at the desk by name.
- At the end of the week, compare the two. If the new method has almost everyone, retire the register.

## Use the data every week

Attendance tracking only pays off if someone acts on it. Set a fixed 20 minutes, say Monday morning:

1. **List members not seen in 7 days** who joined in the last 60 days. A trainer messages each one personally.
2. **List members not seen in 14 days** whose plan ends this month. These are your likely non-renewals.
3. **Look at busy hours.** If 6–8 pm is packed, move a trainer's shift or suggest quieter times to new members.
4. **Note new members' visit counts** for their first month.

The follow-up messages matter more than the software. Our guide on [how to stop gym members dropping out](/guides/how-to-reduce-gym-member-dropouts/) has wording and a 30-day plan.

## Privacy

Attendance records are personal data. Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, you should:

- **Tell members** what you record (name, visit times, and anything biometric) and why.
- **Ask for consent**, especially for fingerprints or face scans, and offer a non-biometric option.
- **Collect only what you need.** Visit times and names are enough for most gyms.
- **Limit access.** Not every staff member needs to see every member's history.
- **Delete data** you no longer need, for example some time after a member leaves.

This is general guidance, not legal advice.

## Cost

Costs vary widely, from a free spreadsheet to software with monthly fees plus hardware. Before you pay for anything, ask:

- Does it show members who have **stopped coming**, not just who came today?
- Can staff check someone in **by name** when the phone or scanner fails?
- Will it work on the **phones and internet** you already have?
- Can you **export your data** if you switch later?
