People spend anywhere from 20 minutes to two hours at the gym, and both can work or fail. The length of the session is the result of what you do in it. Get the content right and the time sorts itself out.

## How much exercise you need in a week

The World Health Organization recommends that adults do:

- **150 to 300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week**, such as brisk walking or cycling, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity, and
- **muscle-strengthening activity on 2 or more days a week**, working all the major muscle groups.

Three strength sessions of 45 to 60 minutes, plus some walking on other days, covers this for most people. A daily 30-minute walk to the office or after dinner counts towards the aerobic part.

## Where the time goes

A strength session is mostly sets and rest. Here is a typical 60-minute beginner session:

| Part | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Warm-up | 8–10 minutes |
| 5 exercises x 3 sets, about 45 seconds per set | about 11 minutes |
| Rest: 1.5 minutes between sets | about 20 minutes |
| Moving between exercises, setting up weights | 8–10 minutes |
| Cool-down and stretching | 5 minutes |
| **Total** | **about 55–60 minutes** |

Notice that the actual lifting is only about 11 minutes. Rest and setup take the rest. That is why sessions stretch when the gym is crowded or when you scroll between sets.

## A simple way to work out your session length

**Minutes ≈ warm-up + (number of sets x (set time + rest)) + cool-down**

Example: 15 sets, about 45 seconds a set and 90 seconds rest:
15 x 2.25 minutes = about 34 minutes, plus 10 for warm-up and 5 for cool-down, makes about 50 minutes.

If you want a shorter session, the levers are clear: fewer exercises, fewer sets, or shorter rests on lighter exercises.

## Session plans for 30, 45 and 60 minutes

### 30 minutes (busy day)

- Warm-up: 5 minutes, including a light first set of your first exercise
- Goblet squat: 3 x 10
- Lat pulldown: 3 x 10
- Dumbbell bench press: 3 x 10
- Rest 60 to 90 seconds. Pair exercises: do a set of squats, then a set of pulldowns, then rest.

### 45 minutes (standard)

- Warm-up: 8 minutes
- Squat or leg press: 3 x 8–10
- Dumbbell bench press: 3 x 8–10
- Seated cable row: 3 x 10
- Romanian deadlift: 3 x 10
- Plank: 2 x 30 seconds
- Cool-down: 3 minutes

### 60 minutes (when you have time)

- Warm-up: 10 minutes
- The 45-minute plan above
- Add one shoulder exercise (3 x 10) and one arm exercise (2 x 12)
- 5 minutes of easy cycling and stretching to finish

## How long to rest between sets

Rest is the part people get wrong in both directions: rushing heavy sets or chatting for five minutes.

| Exercise type | Rest |
| --- | --- |
| Heavy squats, deadlifts, bench press | 2–3 minutes |
| Most machine and dumbbell exercises | 1–2 minutes |
| Small exercises (curls, raises, abs) | 45–90 seconds |

A study in trained men found that longer rests of about 3 minutes led to more strength and muscle gain than 1-minute rests on the big lifts. For beginners on lighter weights, 1 to 2 minutes is usually enough. If your next set falls apart, you need more rest.

## When a longer session is fine

- You are doing a strength session and cardio on the same day.
- You are an advanced lifter with more sets on heavy lifts.
- You are training with a partner and taking turns, which naturally adds rest.

## When a long session is a warning sign

- You spend more time on your phone than lifting.
- You do 8 or more exercises because you are not sure which ones matter.
- You feel drained for the rest of the day, or dread going.
- You are always waiting for machines at peak hours.

## Saving time at a busy gym

Evenings between about 6 and 9 pm are rush hour in many Indian gyms. If that is your only slot:

- **Plan your session before you arrive**, including a backup for each exercise if the machine is busy.
- **Use dumbbells and cables** rather than waiting for the one squat rack.
- **Pair exercises** that use the same spot, like dumbbell press and dumbbell row.
- **Log your sets** so you do not waste time remembering last week's weights.

Our guide on [how many sets and reps to do](/guides/how-many-sets-and-reps-to-build-muscle/) helps you decide how many sets you actually need.

## Recovery counts too

Shorter, regular sessions beat long, rare ones. Three 45-minute sessions a week, every week, will take you further than one 2-hour session when you feel motivated. Leave at least one rest day between hard sessions for the same muscles, and sleep 7 hours or more when you can.

If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure or another health issue, ask your doctor how much and how hard you should exercise.
