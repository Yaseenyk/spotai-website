The deadlift has a reputation. Some people call it the best exercise in the gym; others are scared it will hurt their back. Both views miss the point for a beginner. The deadlift is just picking something up from the floor with good technique, and you learn it in stages.

## What the deadlift works

A deadlift trains the back of your body: glutes, hamstrings, the muscles along your spine, your upper back and your grip. It also teaches the hip hinge, the same pattern you use to lift a bucket of water, a gas cylinder or a child off the floor.

## Learn the hinge before the bar

The hinge means bending at the hips while your back stays long and your shins stay mostly upright. Most beginners squat the weight up instead, or round their back to reach the floor.

**Wall drill.** Stand with your heels about one foot-length from a wall. Soften your knees, then push your hips back until your bum touches the wall. Keep your chest proud and your back flat. Stand tall by squeezing your glutes. Do 2 sets of 10.

**Stick drill.** Hold a PVC pipe or broom along your back so it touches your head, upper back and tailbone. Hinge forward. If the stick leaves any of those three points, you are rounding.

Spend one or two sessions on these. They feel easy, and that is the point.

## Which variation to start with

| Variation | Why it suits beginners | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dumbbell or kettlebell Romanian deadlift | Light, easy to set up, teaches the hinge | Do not reach for the floor; stop when hamstrings are stretched |
| Trap bar deadlift | Handles at your sides, back stays more upright | Not every gym has one |
| Barbell deadlift from blocks | Bar starts higher, so less range to control | Use stable plates or blocks, not a wobbly stack |
| Conventional barbell deadlift | The standard lift | Needs the bar at the right height (see below) |

A full-size 20 kg plate puts the bar about mid-shin. Many gyms only have small iron plates, which leave an empty or lightly loaded bar too low for a beginner to reach with a flat back. Raise the bar on a pair of plates or aerobic steps until it sits where a big plate would put it.

## The 5-step barbell setup

1. **Feet under the bar.** Stand with the bar over the middle of your feet, feet about hip-width apart, toes slightly out.
2. **Grip.** Hinge down and grab the bar just outside your legs, palms facing you.
3. **Shins to the bar.** Bend your knees until your shins touch the bar. Do not move the bar.
4. **Chest up, back flat.** Lift your chest and pull your shoulders slightly back, as if bending the bar around your shins. Take a breath into your belly and brace.
5. **Push the floor away.** Drive through your whole foot, keep the bar sliding up your legs, and stand tall. Lower it the same way: hips back first, then knees bend once the bar passes them.

Each rep starts from a still bar. Reset your breath on the floor between reps rather than bouncing.

## A 6-week beginner plan

Do this once or twice a week, early in your session, after a warm-up. "RIR" means reps in reserve: how many more clean reps you could have done.

| Week | Exercise | Sets x reps | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hinge drills + dumbbell RDL | 3 x 10 | Very easy, 4+ RIR |
| 2 | Dumbbell RDL | 3 x 10 | 3–4 RIR |
| 3 | Barbell deadlift from blocks | 3 x 8 | 3–4 RIR |
| 4 | Barbell deadlift from blocks | 3 x 8 | Add 2.5–5 kg if form held |
| 5 | Barbell deadlift (lower blocks or from floor) | 3 x 6 | 2–3 RIR |
| 6 | Barbell deadlift | 3 x 5 | Add 2.5–5 kg if form held |

This fits the ACSM's general advice for beginners: moderate loads for around 8–12 reps, two to three strength sessions a week, and small increases as you adapt. Warm up properly first; our guide on [how to warm up before lifting](/guides/how-to-warm-up-before-the-gym/) has a routine that works for deadlift days.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rounded lower back | Back curves like a cat at the start | Lighter weight, raise the bar, brace before you pull |
| Bar drifting forward | Bar swings away from shins | Bar over mid-foot, pull it into your legs |
| Hips shooting up first | Legs straighten, back does the lift | Chest up, push the floor, hips and shoulders rise together |
| Leaning back at the top | Hips thrust past the bar, back arched | Stand tall and stop; squeeze glutes |
| Jerking the bar | Slack in arms, sudden pull | Pull the slack out of the bar before you lift |

## Film one set from the side

You cannot see your own back while you deadlift. Prop your phone at hip height, side-on, about two metres away, and record your top set. Pause at the start and at knee height. Look for a flat back and a bar that stays close. ## When to stop

Normal muscle soreness a day or two later is expected. Sharp pain, pain that runs down your leg, or pain that lasts more than a few days is not. Stop deadlifting and see a doctor or physiotherapist.
