Idli is one of the few Indian foods with a standard shape, so it should be easy to count. The catch is size. A small idli from a steamer plate at home, and one from a restaurant, can be very different weights, and a count of "3 idli" means little until you know that. This guide gives the calories per idli, per 100 g, and for the plate with sambar and chutney, with the gram weights shown so you can adjust.

## Calories in idli per 100 g and per piece

The ICMR-NIN Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017) cover raw foods only, so they have the ingredients (see below) but not the steamed idli. The idli figure here comes from the USDA survey food database (FNDDS 2021 to 2023), which has an entry for idli and a portion weight.

| Idli (USDA FNDDS) | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Per 100 g | 128 kcal, 6.36 g protein, 0.35 g fat, 25 g carbohydrate |
| 1 idli (USDA portion) | 38 g |

From that:

| Number of idli (38 g each) | Weight | Calories | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 38 g | about 49 kcal | 2.4 g |
| 2 | 76 g | about 97 kcal | 4.8 g |
| 3 | 114 g | about 146 kcal | 7.3 g |
| 4 | 152 g | about 195 kcal | 9.7 g |

The USDA lists 76 g for "quantity not specified", which is the weight of two idlis. If your idlis are 50 g each, multiply by 1.3; if they are 30 g, by 0.8. The only way to know is to weigh one once on a kitchen scale. Steamed idli has almost no fat, so the number changes with size, not with the cook.

## Where idli protein comes from

An idli batter is usually parboiled rice and urad dal. IFCT 2017 gives raw parboiled rice at about 352 kcal and 7.81 g of protein per 100 g, and raw urad dal (black gram) at about 324 kcal and 23.06 g of protein per 100 g. The urad dal is the likely reason steamed idli carries more protein per 100 g than boiled rice, which USDA lists at about 2.7 g. We do not know your batter ratio, so we use the USDA total for the finished idli.

## Idli with sambar and chutney

Most people do not eat idli alone. Here is a plate with the sides added, using these values:

- **Sambar.** USDA's survey database lists "Sambar, vegetable stew" at 86 kcal and 4.33 g of protein per 100 g. Half a cup is 124 g, so about 107 kcal and 5.4 g of protein. A full cup (248 g) is about 213 kcal.
- **Coconut chutney.** No table gives coconut chutney as a dish. We add up the coconut alone: IFCT 2017 lists fresh coconut kernel at 1,711 kJ, about 409 kcal and 3.8 g of protein per 100 g. 20 g of coconut (our assumption) is about 82 kcal and 0.8 g of protein. Roasted chana dal, oil for a tadka and chillies add a little more.

| Plate | Idli | Sambar | Coconut chutney | Total | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 idli | 97 kcal | 107 kcal (half a cup) | 82 kcal | about 286 kcal | about 11 g |
| 3 idli | 146 kcal | 107 kcal | 82 kcal | about 335 kcal | about 13.5 g |
| 4 idli | 195 kcal | 107 kcal | 82 kcal | about 384 kcal | about 16 g |

The idli is the smaller part of each plate. Half the calories in the 3-idli plate come from the sambar and chutney. If you want to cut or add, adjust the chutney and the sambar first. A tablespoon of ghee or a spoon of podi with oil on top adds more: a teaspoon of ghee is about 37 kcal (USDA).

The USDA sambar entry is a standard American survey recipe with 2.7 g of fat per 100 g. Sambar at home can be thinner (less) or have more dal and oil (more). Treat the protein figure for sambar in particular as rough.

## What the NIN guidelines say about idli portions

The National Institute of Nutrition's manual, *Dietary Guidelines for Indians* (2011), lists idli among its breakfast options in the sample meal plans: 3 idlis in the plan for a sedentary woman and 4 in the plan for a sedentary man, alongside other options such as dosa and upma. That tells you what a typical portion looked like to the authors. It does not give weights, so the 38 g per idli remains the USDA's figure and our assumption.

## How to count your own idli

1. Weigh one steamed idli. Write the number down.
2. Multiply by 128 kcal per 100 g for calories and by 6.36 g per 100 g for protein.
3. Add the sambar by the katori (about 124 g for half a cup, our unit conversion, 86 kcal per 100 g) and the chutney by the spoon.
4. Add ghee or oil on top at about 37 to 40 kcal a teaspoon.

For how to count dishes made for a family, see [how to count calories in Indian home food](/guides/how-to-count-calories-in-indian-home-food/). If you have rice at another meal, [calories in rice](/guides/calories-in-rice/) shows how the same method works there, and [protein in dal per 100 g](/guides/protein-in-dal-per-100g/) explains the dal side of sambar.

All of these are estimates. Portions and recipes vary, and this is food data, not diet advice.
