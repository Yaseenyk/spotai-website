One roti does not have one calorie number, and that is why the first three search results often disagree. Size, the amount of atta in the dough, and what goes on top each change it. This guide shows the number two ways: from the weight of raw atta (the most repeatable method) and from the size of the finished roti. It also covers chapati with ghee, a phulka and a multigrain roti.

## Calories in one roti, from the atta

The ICMR-NIN Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017) give whole wheat atta 1,340 kJ per 100 g, which is 320 kcal, with 10.57 g of protein and 1.53 g of fat. Convert at 4.184 kJ per kcal. Multiply by the grams of atta in the dough ball, and you have the roti, before any ghee. The water in the dough adds weight, not calories, and a bit of dry flour used for rolling is small enough to ignore.

| Atta in the dough ball | Calories | Protein | Roughly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 g | about 64 kcal | 2.1 g | small phulka |
| 25 g | about 80 kcal | 2.6 g | phulka |
| 30 g | about 96 kcal | 3.2 g | everyday roti |
| 40 g | about 128 kcal | 4.2 g | large roti |
| 50 g | about 160 kcal | 5.3 g | very large or thick roti |

The labels in the last column are our guess at what the weights look like. Atta ball weights differ from house to house, so this is the part worth measuring. Weigh the dough balls for one meal, divide, and use that number from then on. The method is in [how to count calories in Indian home food](/guides/how-to-count-calories-in-indian-home-food/).

## Calories in one roti, from the finished size

USDA's survey food database (FNDDS 2021 to 2023) has an entry for "Bread, chappatti or roti" at 299 kcal and 7.85 g of protein per 100 g, with portion weights by size. Multiply the portion by that value:

| Size (USDA) | Weight | Calories | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small, 6 inch | 27 g | about 81 kcal | 2.1 g |
| Medium, 7 inch | 40 g | about 120 kcal | 3.1 g |
| Large, 8 inch | 52 g | about 155 kcal | 4.1 g |

This entry has 9.2 g of fat per 100 g, which suggests an American survey recipe made with some added fat. A dry-roasted home phulka with no oil will land closer to the atta-based table above. Two methods, two numbers, and the gap between them is mostly fat. For a plain roti with no oil, the atta method is the better guide. USDA also lists a packaged plain roti at 297 kcal per 100 g (a 68 g piece is about 202 kcal) and a frozen whole wheat roti at 299 kcal per 100 g (a 43 g piece is about 129 kcal), which shows how much store-bought sizes differ.

## Chapati with ghee

Ghee is the part people forget. USDA lists butter oil (ghee) at 876 kcal per 100 g, and a tablespoon is 12.8 g. A teaspoon, a third of a tablespoon, is about 4.3 g and about 37 kcal.

| Roti | Ghee on top | Calories |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 g atta roti (96 kcal) | none | about 96 kcal |
| 30 g atta roti | 1 teaspoon | about 133 kcal |
| 30 g atta roti | 2 teaspoons | about 170 kcal |
| Medium 40 g roti (120 kcal) | 1 teaspoon | about 157 kcal |

If you spread ghee on both sides and also roast the roti on ghee, the total is higher again. Measure your teaspoon once rather than guessing, because a "light brush" can be one teaspoon or three.

## Phulka vs roti vs chapati

These three names describe the same dough. A phulka is usually smaller and puffed directly over the flame, with no oil. A roti or chapati is often larger and may be cooked on the tawa with a little oil or ghee. The difference in calories comes from the weight of atta and the fat added, not from the name. A 25 g phulka is about 80 kcal. A 40 g roti with a teaspoon of ghee is about 157 kcal.

## Multigrain roti calories

A multigrain atta is a blend, and blends differ by brand and house. IFCT 2017 gives these for single flours, per 100 g, and per 30 g of flour (an everyday roti):

| Flour | kcal per 100 g | Protein per 100 g | 30 g of flour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wheat atta | 320 | 10.6 g | about 96 kcal, 3.2 g protein |
| Ragi | 321 | 7.2 g | about 96 kcal, 2.1 g protein |
| Jowar | 334 | 10.0 g | about 100 kcal, 3.0 g protein |
| Bajra | 348 | 11.0 g | about 104 kcal, 3.3 g protein |
| Maize (dry) | 334 | 8.8 g | about 100 kcal, 2.6 g protein |

So a multigrain roti of 30 g flour is about 96 to 104 kcal. Multigrain is not a lower-calorie bread by this data. If your atta is a mix, weigh it as flour and use around 100 kcal per 30 g.

## How to use this

- Count by the weight of atta if you can: it is the steadiest number.
- If you count by size, use USDA's small, medium and large, and remember they include some fat.
- Add ghee or oil on top at 37 kcal per teaspoon.
- Pair with the rice numbers in [calories in rice](/guides/calories-in-rice/) if you eat both at a meal.
- For protein, see the [highest protein foods in India](/guides/highest-protein-foods-in-india/). A roti gives about 3 g, so it is a carbohydrate food that adds a little protein, not a protein food.

All figures are estimates from food composition tables, not from your kitchen. Portions and recipes vary. This page is not diet advice; if you have a medical condition, ask a doctor or dietitian how many rotis suit you.
