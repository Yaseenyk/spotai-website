If you do the same workout with the same weights for months, your body has no reason to change. Progressive overload is the fix: you ask a little more of your muscles over time. It is the main idea behind every good strength or muscle-building programme.

## Why it works

Training is a stress. Your body responds by getting a little stronger and, over time, building muscle, so the same stress is easier next time. Once it is easy, it stops being a reason to adapt. So you raise the challenge, a small step at a time.

The key word is **small**. Big jumps lead to bad form, missed reps and sometimes injury.

## Five ways to progress

| Method | Example | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add reps | 10 kg dumbbell press: 8 reps → 10 reps | Beginners, dumbbells, machines |
| Add weight | Squat 40 kg → 42.5 kg | Barbell lifts, once reps are maxed |
| Add a set | 3 sets → 4 sets | When weight and reps are stuck |
| Control the tempo | Lower over 3 seconds instead of 1 | Learning form, light weights |
| Improve range | Half squat → squat to parallel | When form is limiting you |

Start with the first two. They are the easiest to measure.

## Double progression: the simplest method

This is how most beginners should apply progressive overload.

1. **Pick a rep range**, for example 8 to 12.
2. **Pick a weight** you can lift for 8 good reps on every set.
3. **Each session, try to add a rep** or two to some sets.
4. **When you hit 12 reps on all sets**, add the smallest weight jump.
5. **Drop back to 8 reps** with the new weight and repeat.

### Worked example: dumbbell shoulder press, 3 sets

| Week | Weight | Reps (set 1, 2, 3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 7.5 kg | 8, 8, 7 |
| 2 | 7.5 kg | 10, 9, 8 |
| 3 | 7.5 kg | 11, 10, 10 |
| 4 | 7.5 kg | 12, 12, 12 |
| 5 | 10 kg | 8, 7, 7 |
| 6 | 10 kg | 9, 8, 8 |

Notice that week 5 looks like a step back in reps. That is normal. The weight is about 33 percent heavier, because many gyms stock dumbbells only in 2.5 kg steps. With small dumbbells that jump is big, so expect several weeks at each weight.

## How much weight to add

The American College of Sports Medicine suggests increasing the load by about **2 to 10 percent** once you can do one or two reps more than your target on two sessions in a row. The NSCA calls this the "2-for-2 rule".

In practice:

- **Barbell squat, deadlift:** add 2.5 to 5 kg in total.
- **Barbell bench press, rows:** add 2.5 kg in total (1.25 kg plates on each side).
- **Dumbbells:** move to the next dumbbell, and use the rep range to bridge the gap.
- **Machines:** move one pin down the stack.

## When you get stuck

Every lifter stalls. Before changing your programme, check these:

- **Sleep:** fewer than seven hours most nights?
- **Food:** enough calories and protein? See [how much protein you need per day](/guides/how-much-protein-per-day-for-gym/).
- **Rest:** enough rest days, and 48 hours before training the same muscles hard again? See [how many rest days you need](/guides/rest-days-how-many-per-week/).
- **Form:** are reps getting shorter or sloppier to hit the number?

If all of that is fine and you have been stuck for three or four weeks:

1. **Take a lighter week.** Use about 60 to 70 percent of your usual weights for the same exercises. Many people come back stronger.
2. **Change the rep range.** Move from 8–12 to 6–8 for a few weeks.
3. **Add a set** to the stuck exercise.
4. **Swap the exercise** for a close variation, for example barbell bench press to dumbbell bench press.

## Progress needs good form

Adding weight to a squat where your knees cave in or your chest drops is not progress. Keep the form the same as the weight goes up. If you are not sure, film a set and compare it with an earlier one. For squats, the SpotAI coach flags knee cave, shallow depth and forward lean on each rep.

## Write it down

You cannot beat last week's numbers if you do not remember them. Use a notebook or a notes app, or log sets in SpotAI, which keeps your history and personal bests. Before each session, look at what you did last time and decide which set you will beat.
