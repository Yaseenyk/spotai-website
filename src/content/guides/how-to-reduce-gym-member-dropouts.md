Most gyms put their effort into getting new members through the door. Keeping them is cheaper, and the work happens in the first few weeks. Here is a practical plan any gym can run, with or without software.

## Why members drop out

When members leave, the reasons are usually simple:

- **They do not know what to do.** Without a plan, the gym feels intimidating and they drift to the treadmill, then stop.
- **They do not see progress.** Weeks pass, the mirror looks the same, and motivation fades.
- **Nobody notices.** If no one asks where they have been, it is easy to stop.
- **Life gets in the way.** Exams, travel, festivals and work changes break the routine, and there is no nudge to restart.

Price matters too, but in most gyms it is not the first reason. People who feel they are improving, and who feel known, tend to renew.

## A 30-day plan for every new member

### Day 1: a plan, not just a tour

- Give them a **simple written plan** for the first two weeks: three days a week, five or six exercises, sets and reps.
- Have a trainer **show each exercise once** and check their form.
- Write down **one goal** in their words: lose 5 kg, get stronger, feel fitter.

### Week 1: build the check-in habit

- Ask them to **check in every visit**, by QR, app or register, so you have data from day one.
- A trainer says hello by name on their second or third visit. Small, and it matters.

### Week 2: the first check-in conversation

- Ask how the plan feels. Too easy, too hard, confusing?
- Adjust one thing. Members who see the plan change for them feel looked after.

### Week 3–4: catch the quiet ones

- Every week, list members who **joined in the last 60 days and have not checked in for 7 days**.
- Send a **personal message** from a trainer, not a broadcast: "Hi Priya, haven't seen you this week. Shall I book you for a quick form check on Thursday?"
- Offer something useful (a new plan, a session time, a training partner), not a discount.

### Day 30: show the progress

- Show them what changed: visits this month, weights going up, a form check compared with week one.
- Set the goal for the next month.

## What to do every week as an owner

| Check | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Members not seen in 7 days | Early warning, while they can still be brought back |
| Members not seen in 14 days, plan ending this month | Highest risk of not renewing |
| New members' visits in their first month | Shows whether onboarding is working |
| Busiest hours | Crowding at peak times pushes people away |

## Ideas that help members stay

- **Pair beginners** who train at the same time. A training partner is a strong reason to show up.
- **Run monthly challenges** based on visits, not just weight lost, so everyone can take part.
- **Plan for festival and exam seasons.** Message members before Diwali or exams with a short "keep it going" routine rather than waiting for them to disappear.
- **Celebrate milestones**: 25th visit, first month done, a new personal best.

## Keep it light

None of this needs a big team. One trainer spending 20 minutes a day on check-ins and messages is often enough for a neighbourhood gym. The important part is doing it every week, and noticing members before their renewal date rather than after.
