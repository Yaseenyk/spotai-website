What you feel during a lift and what you actually do are often different. A rep that felt deep might have stopped a hand above parallel. Your knees might drift in only on the last two reps. A 30-second video catches what you cannot see in the mirror.

## Set up your phone

1. **Height.** Place the phone at about hip height. Use a bench, a stack of plates, a water bottle or a cheap tripod. Filming from the floor or from head height distorts angles.
2. **Distance.** About 2 to 3 metres away, so your whole body and the weight fit in the frame with some space around you.
3. **Orientation.** Landscape for most lifts. Portrait works for a squat or deadlift filmed side-on if you are close.
4. **Level.** Keep the phone straight, not tilted. A tilted phone makes a straight back look bent.
5. **Light.** Avoid filming against a bright window. You will be a dark shape.
6. **Start recording before your first rep.** Stop after you rack the weight.

## Which angle for which lift

| Lift | Main angle | What it shows | Second angle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | Side | Depth, back angle, bar path, heels | Front, for knees caving in |
| Deadlift | Side | Back position, bar staying close, hips rising too early | 45 degrees from the front |
| Bench press | Side, from the foot end at an angle | Bar touch point, elbow angle, bar path | Head end, for even lockout |
| Lat pulldown | Side | Leaning back, elbows, range of motion | Front, for even pulling |
| Rows | Side | Back angle, torso swinging | — |
| Overhead press | Side | Leaning back, bar path over the head | Front, for even arms |
| Lunges | Front | Knee tracking, balance | Side, for depth and torso |

If you can only film one angle, film from the side.

## What to look for, lift by lift

### Squat

- Do your hips go down until the hip crease is at or below the knee?
- Do your heels stay flat?
- Do your knees track over your toes or fall inwards on the way up?
- Does your chest stay up, or does your back angle collapse as you stand?

Our guides on [squat depth](/guides/how-deep-should-you-squat/), [knees caving in](/guides/how-to-fix-knees-caving-in-squat/) and [leaning forward in the squat](/guides/forward-lean-in-squat/) explain each of these faults with drills to fix them.

### Deadlift

- Does the bar stay close to your legs the whole way?
- Do your hips and shoulders rise together, or do your hips shoot up first?
- Does your lower back stay neutral, or does it round as the bar leaves the floor?

### Bench press

- Does the bar touch the same spot on your chest each rep?
- Do your feet stay planted and your hips stay on the bench?
- Do both arms lock out evenly?

### Lat pulldown and rows

- Are you leaning back more and more to move the weight?
- Do your elbows travel down and back, or do your hands do all the work?
- Is the range the same on the last rep as on the first?

## How to review the video

- **Watch once at normal speed** to get a feel for the set.
- **Use slow motion or pause** at the bottom of a rep and at the top.
- **Compare rep 1 with the last rep.** Fatigue shows up as shallower depth, more lean, or knees drifting.
- **Compare with last week.** Same angle, same distance, so you can see real changes.
- **Pick one thing to fix.** Change one cue on the next set, then film again. Trying to fix five things at once rarely works.

Many phones have a built-in slow-motion mode. It is useful for fast lifts, but regular video is fine for most beginners.

## Filming etiquette in a busy gym

- **Film yourself, not others.** Check what else is in the frame before you post anything.
- **Do not block walkways** or someone else's bench with your phone or tripod.
- **Keep it quick at peak hours.** Set up, film one set, move on.
- **Never film in changing rooms or washrooms.**
- **Follow your gym's rules.** Some gyms ask members not to film, or not in certain areas.

## What apps can and cannot do

Some apps now use the phone camera to track your body and give feedback on your reps. They can be helpful, but check exactly what each one covers. SpotAI's coach, for example, works on squats only today: it flags knee cave, shallow depth and forward lean per rep. For other lifts, you can film a set in the app and review it yourself.

No app sees everything. Pain, a previous injury or a lift that feels wrong but looks fine on video are good reasons to ask a trainer to watch you in person.

## When to get help

- If the same fault keeps appearing after a few weeks of trying to fix it, ask a qualified trainer to look at your video.
- If you feel pain during or after a lift, especially sharp pain or pain that lingers, stop that exercise and see a doctor or physiotherapist.
