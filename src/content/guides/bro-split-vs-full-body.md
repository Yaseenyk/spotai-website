Walk into many Indian gyms as a beginner and you will be handed the same plan: Monday chest, Tuesday back, Wednesday shoulders, Thursday legs, Friday arms. That is a bro split. Ask online and you will hear that full body is better. Here is what actually matters.

## What each one looks like

**Bro split (one body part per day)**

| Day | Muscle group |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Chest |
| Tuesday | Back |
| Wednesday | Shoulders |
| Thursday | Legs |
| Friday | Arms |
| Saturday–Sunday | Rest |

Each muscle is trained hard **once a week**, with lots of sets in that one session.

**Full body (whole body every session)**

| Day | Session |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Full body A |
| Wednesday | Full body B |
| Friday | Full body A |
| Other days | Rest or walking |

Each muscle is trained **two or three times a week**, with fewer sets each time.

## What the research says

Two reviews by Brad Schoenfeld and colleagues are the most useful here:

- **2016**: across the studies they reviewed, training a muscle **at least twice a week** led to more growth than training it once a week.
- **2019**: when the total number of sets per muscle per week was **the same**, how often you trained it made little difference. Frequency mainly helps you fit in more good-quality sets.

So a bro split is not "wrong". It can work when you do enough total sets. The catch is in real life.

## The missed-day problem

Most beginners do not train five days every single week. Work runs late, there are exams, guests at home, a wedding, a festival week.

| You miss... | On a bro split | On full body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One day | That muscle group waits 2 weeks | Every muscle still trained twice that week |
| A whole week | Restart, and the week order breaks | Pick up where you left off |

With full body, a missed session costs you a little. With a bro split, it can cost you a whole muscle group for a fortnight. That is the main reason full body suits most beginners.

## Which one to pick: go by your days

| Days you can reliably train | Best choice |
| --- | --- |
| 2–3 | Full body |
| 4 | Upper/lower (upper, lower, rest, upper, lower) |
| 5–6 | Push/pull/legs, or a bro split if you enjoy it |

Be honest about the number. Pick the plan for the week you actually have, not the week you hope to have.

## A simple 3-day full-body plan

Do 2–3 sets of each exercise. Rest 1–2 minutes between sets.

| Full body A | Reps | Full body B | Reps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goblet squat or leg press | 8–12 | Romanian deadlift (dumbbells) | 8–12 |
| Dumbbell bench press | 8–12 | Lat pulldown | 8–12 |
| Seated cable row | 10–12 | Dumbbell shoulder press | 8–12 |
| Walking lunges | 10 each leg | Leg press or split squat | 10–12 |
| Plank | 20–40 seconds | Dumbbell curl + triceps pushdown | 10–15 |

Alternate A and B: A–B–A one week, B–A–B the next. Add a little weight or a rep when all sets feel clean. For the reasoning behind sets and reps, see [how many sets and reps to build muscle](/guides/how-many-sets-and-reps-to-build-muscle/), and for a complete starting routine, the [gym workout plan for beginners](/guides/gym-workout-plan-for-beginners/).

## The middle path: upper/lower and push/pull/legs

Once you train four or more days a week, or your full-body sessions feel too long, a split makes sense:

- **Upper/lower** over four days trains each muscle twice a week with shorter sessions.
- **Push/pull/legs** groups muscles that work together. Done twice a week over six days, it hits each muscle twice. Read more in [push, pull, legs for beginners](/guides/push-pull-legs-split-for-beginners/).

Both keep the main advantage of full body (each muscle twice a week) while giving more sets per muscle.

## When a bro split makes sense

- You have trained steadily for a year or more and rarely miss sessions.
- You enjoy long, focused sessions on one muscle group.
- You want extra work on a lagging body part and recover well.

Even then, many experienced lifters add a second weekly touch for each muscle, for example a few sets of chest on shoulder day.

## Whatever you choose

- **Log your sets.** If you can see the total sets per muscle per week, you can compare plans honestly. SpotAI's "This week" view shows what you did over the week.
- **Progress slowly.** Small increases in weight or reps every week or two.
- **Rest.** Muscles grow between sessions, not during them.

If you have an injury, or pain in a joint during any exercise, see a doctor or physiotherapist before continuing that exercise.
