Knees drifting inward during a squat is one of the most common things trainers correct in beginners. The technical name is **knee valgus**: seen from the front, the knee moves inside the line between the hip and the foot.

It is worth fixing early. Squats feel more stable when the knees track over the toes, and good habits are easier to build with light weights than to unlearn later.

## Why your knees cave in

Most of the time it comes down to one of these:

- **Weight on the toes.** When your weight rolls forward and inward, the knees follow.
- **Feet too narrow or pointing straight ahead.** A stance that does not suit your hips leaves less room for the knees.
- **Hips not strong enough yet.** The glutes help hold the thigh in line. When they tire, the knees drift in, often on the way up.
- **Too much weight.** Form that looks fine with an empty bar can break down at a heavier load.

## Fix 1: set your feet first

Stand with your feet about shoulder-width apart and turn your toes out a little, somewhere around 10 to 30 degrees. Spread your weight across three points of each foot: the heel, the base of the big toe and the base of the little toe. Keep all three on the floor for the whole rep.

## Fix 2: use a "knees out" cue

As you go down and especially as you stand up, think about pushing your knees out so they stay over your toes. Some people prefer "spread the floor apart with your feet", which does the same thing.

## Fix 3: put a light band above your knees

Loop a light resistance band just above the knees and do bodyweight squats. The band pulls your knees in, so you have to push out against it. Do two or three sets of 10, then take the band off and squat without it. Most people find the movement feels more natural after a few sessions.

## Fix 4: go lighter and pause at the bottom

Reduce the weight and do **pause squats**: sit down, hold the bottom position for one to two seconds, then stand up while keeping the knees out. The pause takes away the bounce and makes you control the position. Goblet squats with a dumbbell held at your chest are a good choice here.

## Fix 5: train the hips on their own

Add one or two of these at the end of your leg day, two or three sets each:

- **Glute bridges** – 12 to 15 reps
- **Banded side steps** – 10 to 15 steps each way
- **Clamshells** – 12 to 15 reps each side

## Check whether it worked

You cannot see your own knees well while squatting, so check from the outside:

1. Film one set from the front, with the phone at knee height.
2. Watch the slow-motion replay and look at the knees on the way up.
3. If they stay over the toes for every rep, add a little weight next session.
