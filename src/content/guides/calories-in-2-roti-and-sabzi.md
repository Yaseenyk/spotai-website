"Calories in 2 roti and sabzi" is a question with a different answer in every kitchen, because the roti is one number and the sabzi is another, and the sabzi has the oil. This guide fixes the roti at a stated weight, then puts eight common sides next to it with their portion sizes, so you can compare a few meals and see what actually moves the total. All of it comes from named tables, with our assumptions marked.

## The base: 2 rotis

We take a roti made from 30 g of whole wheat atta. The ICMR-NIN Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017) list atta at 1,340 kJ per 100 g, which is 320 kcal at 4.184 kJ per kcal, with 10.57 g of protein. So one roti is about 96 kcal and 3.2 g of protein, and two are about **192 kcal and 6.3 g of protein**, before ghee. The details, other sizes and the USDA numbers are in [calories in roti and chapati](/guides/calories-in-roti-chapati/).

If your rotis are bigger, say USDA's medium 7 inch roti at 40 g (about 120 kcal each), 2 rotis are about 239 kcal, which is about 47 kcal above our base. Add that to each meal below.

## The sabzi: eight portions with their sources

The sabzi is where the numbers differ. We use USDA's survey database (FNDDS 2021 to 2023) for finished dishes, and IFCT 2017 plus USDA oil values for two dishes we add up ourselves. Portions are the ones USDA lists (a cup, or a stated weight); a standard Indian katori is smaller than a US cup, so a katori of a dish is a bit less than a "cup" figure below. We say which is which.

| Side | Portion and weight | Calories | Protein | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vegetable curry | 1 cup, 240 g | about 206 kcal | 3.8 g | USDA, 86 kcal and 1.6 g per 100 g |
| Okra (bhindi), cooked, fat added | 1 cup, 165 g | about 97 kcal | 3.3 g | USDA, 59 kcal and 2.0 g per 100 g |
| Aloo sabzi, our sum | 1 katori: 150 g raw potato, 2 teaspoons oil, 30 g onion | about 199 kcal | 2.8 g | IFCT potato and onion, USDA oil |
| Dal, our sum | 1 katori: 30 g raw masoor dal, 1 teaspoon oil | about 137 kcal | 7.3 g | IFCT dal, USDA oil |
| Palak paneer | 1 cup, 200 g | about 202 kcal | 10.8 g | USDA, 101 kcal and 5.4 g per 100 g |
| Chana (channa saag) | 1 cup, 245 g | about 218 kcal | 10.0 g | USDA, 89 kcal and 4.1 g per 100 g |
| Chicken curry | 1 cup, 240 g | about 257 kcal | 15.6 g | USDA, 107 kcal and 6.5 g per 100 g |

How we added up the two home dishes. Aloo sabzi: raw potato is 292 kJ per 100 g in IFCT, about 70 kcal, so 150 g is about 105 kcal; peanut oil is 884 kcal per 100 g in USDA and a teaspoon is 4.5 g, so 2 teaspoons are about 80 kcal; 30 g of onion adds about 14 kcal. The total is 199 kcal. Dal: 30 g of raw masoor dal at 322 kcal per 100 g is about 97 kcal, plus 1 teaspoon of oil at about 40 kcal. These are recipe sums, not measured dishes, and the weight of potato and oil is our assumption.

## Common roti plus sabzi meals

Putting the base and the sides together:

| Meal | Calories | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 roti + 1 cup vegetable curry | about 398 kcal | about 10 g |
| 2 roti + 1 katori aloo sabzi | about 391 kcal | about 9 g |
| 2 roti + 1 cup okra | about 289 kcal | about 10 g |
| 2 roti + 1 katori dal | about 329 kcal | about 14 g |
| 2 roti + half a cup palak paneer (100 g) | about 293 kcal | about 12 g |
| 2 roti + 1 cup palak paneer | about 394 kcal | about 17 g |
| 2 roti + 1 cup chana | about 410 kcal | about 16 g |
| 2 roti + 1 cup chicken curry | about 449 kcal | about 22 g |

For **1 roti and sabzi**, with the same side: take one roti (96 kcal and 3.2 g) instead of two, so each meal above is about 96 kcal and 3 g lower. For example, 1 roti and a cup of vegetable curry is about 302 kcal, and 1 roti and a katori of dal is about 233 kcal.

## What these numbers say

- **The sabzi decides the total.** Two rotis are always about 192 kcal in this table. The side adds between about 100 and 260 kcal.
- **Oil is most of the variation.** A teaspoon of oil is about 40 kcal. The aloo sabzi at 199 kcal has about 80 kcal from its 2 teaspoons of oil. Using 4 teaspoons instead of 2 adds another 80.
- **Protein depends on dal, paneer, chana or chicken, not the roti.** The roti gives about 3 g each. Dal, paneer, chana and chicken give 7 to 16 g in the portions shown. [Protein in dal per 100 g](/guides/protein-in-dal-per-100g/) shows the dal maths.
- **Portion is the second factor.** A "cup" of USDA vegetable curry is 240 g. A katori from a smaller bowl is less. If your katori holds 150 ml, a cup-based figure is roughly 60 percent too high for it. Weigh a katori of your own sabzi once and scale.

## Add-ons that people forget

Ghee on the roti is about 37 kcal a teaspoon (USDA: butter oil 876 kcal per 100 g, a teaspoon about 4.3 g). Curd, salad and a small helping of rice add more. If rice is part of the meal, see [calories in rice](/guides/calories-in-rice/), and for the choice between rice and roti, [rice vs chapati calories](/guides/rice-vs-chapati-calories/). For a method to count family dishes by the pot, read [how to count calories in Indian home food](/guides/how-to-count-calories-in-indian-home-food/).

All figures are estimates from tables and our assumptions. Real roti sizes, katoris and oil vary, so a result 10 to 20 percent off is normal. This page is food data, not diet advice; if you have a medical condition, ask a doctor or dietitian.
