When people search for the "top 10 protein foods" or "highest protein foods per 100 g", they often get lists built for another country. Here is a list for Indian kitchens, with **vegetarian protein sources** marked and a plain note on what each number means in a real portion.

## Highest protein foods per 100 g (India)

Typical values, worked out from Indian Food Composition Tables figures. Recipes, brands and cooking change them, so treat them as estimates.

| Food | Protein per 100 g | A typical portion | Veg? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soya chunks (dry) | about 52 g | 50 g dry: about 26 g | Yes |
| Peanuts | about 25 g | 30 g: 7 to 8 g | Yes |
| Chicken breast (raw) | 21 to 23 g | 150 g: about 32 to 35 g | No |
| Fish (rohu, surmai, raw) | 17 to 20 g | 150 g: about 26 to 30 g | No |
| Paneer | about 18 g | 100 g: about 18 g | Yes |
| Egg | about 12 g | 1 egg: about 6 g | No (egg) |
| Moong or masoor dal (cooked) | about 5 to 6 g | 1 katori (150 g): 7 to 9 g | Yes |
| Rajma or chana (cooked) | about 5 to 6 g | 1 katori (150 g): 8 to 9 g | Yes |
| Curd | about 3 to 3.5 g | 200 g: 6 to 7 g | Yes |
| Milk | about 3 g | 250 ml: about 8 g | Yes |

Per 100 g is not the same as per meal. Cooked dal looks low per 100 g, yet two full katoris of it is a real contribution. Dry soya chunks look very high, yet you eat them after they swell with water.

## Vegetarian protein sources chart

If you do not eat eggs, fish or chicken, build each meal around one of these:

- **Soya chunks:** the densest source. Add to sabzi or pulao.
- **Paneer and tofu:** bhurji, palak paneer, grilled.
- **Dal, rajma, chana, chole, moong:** the everyday base. Eat larger katoris.
- **Curd, milk, buttermilk:** easy additions to any meal.
- **Peanuts, roasted chana, sprouts:** snacks that carry protein.

## Protein foods list for weight loss

When you are eating less, protein density matters. The best choices per calorie are usually eggs and egg whites, chicken breast, fish, curd, sprouts, dal and soya chunks. Paneer and peanuts are good but calorie-dense, so measure them. The [7-day diet plan for weight loss](/guides/7-day-diet-plan-for-weight-loss/) shows how to put them in a week.

## How much protein you need

| Guideline | Amount | Who it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICMR-NIN RDA | 0.83 g per kg body weight per day | General adult population |
| ISSN position stand | 1.4 to 2.0 g per kg body weight per day | People who train regularly |

For a 60 kg person who lifts, that is roughly 85 to 120 g a day. The [gym diet plan for muscle gain](/guides/gym-diet-plan-for-muscle-gain/) shows how to spread it across meals, about 20 to 40 g at a time.

If you have kidney disease or another medical condition, ask your doctor before raising your protein.
