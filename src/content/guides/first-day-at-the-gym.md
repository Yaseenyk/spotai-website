Walking into a gym for the first time can feel like everyone knows what they are doing except you. They don't: most people there also started as beginners. A little preparation makes day one much easier.

## What to bring

- **Water bottle**
- **Small towel** (many gyms ask you to wipe benches after use)
- **Clean indoor shoes**, if the gym asks for them
- **A lock**, if lockers are not provided
- **Your phone**, for your plan and to film a set if you want to check your form

## Before you go

- **Eat something light 1–2 hours before**: a banana, poha, upma, curd with fruit, or toast with peanut butter.
- **Drink water** through the day.
- **Pick a quiet time** if you can. Late mornings and early afternoons are usually calmer than 6–9 pm.

## When you arrive

1. **Ask for a tour.** Find out where the water, changing rooms, dumbbells and machines are.
2. **Tell a trainer it is your first day.** Ask them to watch you do a squat and a push-up, and to show you two or three machines.
3. **Ask about the rules**: shoe rules, wiping equipment, how long you can use a machine at busy times.

## A 45-minute first workout

**Warm-up (8–10 minutes)**
- 5 minutes on the cycle or a brisk treadmill walk
- 10 arm circles each way, 10 bodyweight squats, 10 hip hinges

**Main part (25–30 minutes): 2 sets of each, light weight, rest 60–90 seconds**

| Exercise | Reps | What it trains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goblet squat (light dumbbell) | 10 | Legs and glutes |
| Lat pulldown | 10–12 | Back |
| Dumbbell shoulder press (seated) | 10 | Shoulders |
| Glute bridge | 12 | Glutes |
| Plank | 20–30 seconds | Core |

Pick a weight that feels easy enough to finish every rep with good form. You should feel like you could do a few more.

**Cool-down (5 minutes)**
- Slow walk, then light stretches for legs, chest and shoulders.

## After the first day

- **Expect some soreness** a day or two later. It usually eases as your body gets used to training.
- **Plan your next visit** for two days later. Three visits a week is a good start.
- **Write down what you did**, so next time you can add a little weight or a few reps.

## Small things that help you keep going

- **Go at the same time each visit.** You will start recognising people, and it becomes a routine.
- **Find a gym buddy** at your level. It is easier to show up when someone expects you.
- **Measure progress beyond the scale**: visits per week, weights going up, how easy the stairs feel.

If you have a health condition, are pregnant, or have not exercised in a long time, check with your doctor before starting a new training routine.
